# Chapter 1 Final Brief — The AI You Are Already Governing

**Status:** Final brief approved for drafting  
**Target length:** 2,800–3,500 words  
**Book role:** Open the reader's field of view before introducing accountability theory.

## Reader entry belief

AI governance begins when the organisation deliberately selects, procures or deploys an AI system.

## Opening contradiction

An executive can believe the organisation has only a small number of AI initiatives while employees are already using public generative AI, enterprise software has acquired AI features through routine updates, and established workflows are increasingly mediated by recommendation, generation or automation.

The organisation may therefore already be governing AI — badly, informally or invisibly — before it has decided that governance has begun.

## Chapter purpose

Move the reader from **tool visibility** to **work visibility**.

The chapter should establish that governance starts by understanding where AI participates in work, decisions and actions, not merely by listing products that carry an AI label.

## Governing argument

AI governance begins with visibility of **use in context**.

A register is necessary but insufficient. The same AI system can present very different risks depending on what people ask it to do, who is affected, what information it touches, and whether it merely assists, recommends, prioritises or acts.

## Reader discovery

The practical unit of governance is not simply the AI product.

It is the **AI use case embedded in organisational work**.

## Evidence to integrate

- NIST AI RMF Map: intended purpose, deployment context, users, impacts, limitations and foreseeable use/misuse.
- Australian Government Guidance for AI Adoption and AI systems register: capture systems, use cases, accountable people, governance level and embedded AI features.
- Australian AI screening tool: proportionate governance and prevention of governance overload.
- Samsung 2023: unsanctioned external GenAI use and sensitive data exposure.
- Cemex 2026: HR agent integrated across Teams, SharePoint, ServiceNow and SAP SuccessFactors.
- Wipro 2026: AI agent embedded into Microsoft 365 workflows and enterprise data.
- Shadow-AI survey evidence only as contextual support, not universal prevalence.

## Evidence boundaries

Do not:
- imply every AI feature is high risk;
- argue that inventories are pointless;
- universalise shadow-AI prevalence;
- use vendor performance claims as causal evidence;
- suggest every use needs bespoke governance.

## Gardner synthesis

Primary line:

> **Govern the work AI participates in, not merely the tools labelled AI.**

Secondary line:

> **Visibility first. Proportionality second. Control according to consequence.**

These are Gardner syntheses. They should emerge from the evidence rather than appear as unsupported slogans.

## Narrative movement

1. Open inside the ordinary organisation, not with regulation.
2. Show how AI enters work without a clean “AI project” boundary.
3. Use Samsung to reveal the visibility problem through informal use.
4. Complicate the picture with embedded enterprise AI: Cemex and Wipro.
5. Establish the limits of tool inventories.
6. Bring in NIST and Australian guidance to show why context/use matters.
7. Introduce proportionality so visibility does not become bureaucracy.
8. Close with the unresolved question: once the organisation can finally see where AI participates in work, **what exactly is governing those uses?**

## Concepts to introduce

- AI use
- embedded AI
- shadow AI
- consequential use
- decision participation

Use terms naturally; avoid textbook-style definition blocks in prose.

## Concepts deliberately withheld

- Accountability–Control Gap
- Meaningful Accountability Test
- Delegated Authority Map
- Governance as Permission
- Evolved Governance Chain

They belong later.

## Repetition risks

Avoid:
- repeated “AI is everywhere” formulations;
- lists of tools;
- generic warnings about ethics;
- over-explaining shadow AI;
- previewing Chapters 3–5.

## Ending requirement

No recap. End on a consequence or question that makes Chapter 2 necessary.
