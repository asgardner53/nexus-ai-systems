## Purpose

This register records material incidents involving Nexus AI Studios, connected systems, controlled documents, AIRBOK development, assessment review, privacy, security, evidence integrity, publication or human oversight.

The register is intended to ensure that incidents are identified, contained, corrected, reviewed and used to strengthen controls.

## Incident categories

Use one or more of the following categories:

- Privacy or confidentiality
- Unauthorised access
- Unsupported or incorrect output
- Fabricated evidence or citation
- Autonomous or unauthorised decision
- Incorrect competency recommendation
- Repository or document-control failure
- AIRBOK doctrine or architecture control failure
- Publication-status error
- Connector misuse
- Data loss or overwrite
- Bias, unfairness or harmful output
- Failure of human review
- Other material governance failure

## Severity levels

| Severity | Definition | Typical response |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Limited impact, no sensitive data exposure and no formal decision affected | Correct, record and monitor |
| Moderate | Material error, control weakness or limited disclosure requiring formal correction | Contain, notify owner, investigate and implement corrective action |
| High | Significant privacy, assessment, governance, publication or repository impact | Suspend affected workflow, preserve records, notify relevant authority and undertake formal review |
| Critical | Serious harm, major breach, systemic failure or widespread unauthorised disclosure or decision | Immediate suspension, executive escalation, legal or regulatory review where required, and controlled recovery |

## Mandatory incident information

Each future incident entry must record:

1. date and time identified;
2. affected Studio, connector, repository or document;
3. incident category and severity;
4. concise factual summary;
5. affected people, records or outputs;
6. immediate containment action;
7. responsible incident owner;
8. investigation findings;
9. corrective and preventive actions;
10. whether any output, decision or publication was corrected or withdrawn;
11. whether any person, client, RTO, regulator or other authority was notified;
12. closure decision and date;
13. related commit, issue, pull request or controlled record where applicable.

## Immediate response process

When a material incident is identified:

1. stop or restrict the affected workflow;
2. preserve relevant files, prompts, outputs, logs and repository history;
3. notify Alec Gardner and any relevant accountable owner;
4. assess the people, information, decisions and publications affected;
5. correct, withdraw or quarantine inaccurate outputs;
6. determine whether access, connectors or automated actions must be suspended;
7. assess notification, contractual, legal, regulatory or RTO obligations;
8. implement corrective action;
9. document the incident and decision trail;
10. review whether Studio instructions, control records or registers require amendment.

## Studio-specific triggers

### Assessment Review Studio

Record an incident where there is:

- disclosure of student information;
- invented or unreviewed evidence;
- an outcome communicated without assessor approval;
- use of the wrong assessment instrument or marking guide;
- failure to review required video evidence;
- an unsupported Competent or Not Yet Competent recommendation;
- loss or unauthorised storage of assessment records.

### AIRBOK Development Studio

Record an incident where there is:

- fabricated or materially incorrect citation;
- draft doctrine presented as approved;
- unauthorised change to the locked proposition or 18-chapter architecture;
- overwrite or loss of controlling manuscript content;
- use of an outdated manuscript as the controlling version;
- draft standards, assessments or credentials presented as formally approved;
- publication without the required author approval.

### GitHub and document control

Record an incident where there is:

- unauthorised repository access;
- sensitive information stored in a public repository;
- deletion or overwrite of controlled material without authority;
- incorrect document status;
- loss of traceability between approval and the authoritative file;
- material change made outside the approved workflow.

## Privacy controls

This register must not contain unnecessary personal information, student evidence, confidential client content, passwords, secret keys or security details that would create additional risk.

Sensitive incident evidence must be retained in an approved restricted system. The register should contain only the minimum information needed for governance and traceability.

## Closure requirements

An incident may only be closed when:

- containment is complete;
- affected outputs or records have been corrected or withdrawn;
- required notifications have been completed;
- root cause has been considered;
- corrective actions have been assigned or completed;
- relevant governance documents have been updated where needed;
- the accountable owner has approved closure.

## Review and reporting

The register must be reviewed:

- after every High or Critical incident;
- at least every six months;
- before approving material expansion of automated action authority;
- when a pattern of Low or Moderate incidents suggests a systemic weakness.
