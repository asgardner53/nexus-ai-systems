# Concept Provenance Register

| Concept | Origin/status | Neighbouring scholarship / prior art | Conceptual gap | Exact claim permitted now | Claims excluded now | Hostile evidence / challenge | Revision trigger | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI Governance Operating System | Gardner organising metaphor — **not original as a label** | ISO/IEC 42001 AI management system; NIST AI RMF; several 2025–26 commercial platforms explicitly use “AI Governance Operating System”; 2026 research uses related “Governance Operating System” terminology | Our contribution is not the phrase but the specific organisational integration model linking strategy, decision authority, risk, data, controls, human judgement, capability, evidence and assurance | Use “operating system” as a disclosed metaphor for an integrated organisational governance model, provided the book differentiates it from software products and management-system standards | Do not claim first use, proprietary naming, empirical validation, or uniqueness of “AI Governance Operating System” | The metaphor may be read as software/platform language or as repackaged ISO management-system thinking | If architecture does not add a materially distinct organisational model beyond existing frameworks | REFRAME — retain metaphor, abandon naming originality |
| Evolved Governance Chain | **Gardner book-level synthesis — evidence PASS; awaiting author provenance decision** | IAASB assurance standards; PCAOB audit evidence; COSO internal control and monitoring; IIA Three Lines and Global Standards; ISO/IEC 42001, ISO/IEC 42006 and ISO 19011; NIST Govern/Map/Measure/Manage; UK and Australian AI assurance guidance; EU AI Act logging and post-market monitoring; algorithmic auditing, decision provenance, reviewability, lifecycle assurance and continuous auditing | Integrate the book’s Meaningful Accountability architecture into a practical traceability spine linking purpose, authorised authority, boundary, execution, evidence, evaluation and learning while treating accountability, risk, human judgement, controls, monitoring and change as cross-cutting conditions | Subject to Alec approval: “The Evolved Governance Chain is a diagnostic traceability spine linking Purpose → Authority → Boundary → Execution → Evidence → Evaluation → Learning. Assurance is the conclusion produced by evaluating a defined governance claim and the relevant spine against suitable criteria and sufficient, appropriate evidence.” | Do not claim a universal lifecycle, causal law, mandatory sequence, maturity model, audit standard, certification method, external validation, complete reconstructability or proof that an AI use is safe, fair, lawful, trustworthy or effective | “Chain” may imply false linearity; existing ISO/NIST/COSO/assurance cycles provide substantial prior art; evidence and controls are concurrent; a complete record can preserve a poor decision; auditability can privilege what is measurable over judgement and affected-party evidence | If Alec rejects the name or spine; if the manuscript treats assurance as a chain link; if cross-cutting conditions become sequential boxes; if prior art duplicates the complete integration; if the chain adds no diagnostic value beyond existing frameworks | CONDITIONAL PASS — retain only as a non-linear diagnostic traceability spine after Alec approval |
| Decision Rights Before Controls | **Established neighbouring idea; Gardner phrasing not distinctive** | Weill & Ross IT governance; MIT CISR 2026 Designing Decision Rights for AI; contemporary AI accountability frameworks | Stronger contribution lies beyond allocation of rights: preserving alignment among authority, capability, evidence access, intervention capacity and accountability | State that explicit decision rights are a foundational governance requirement | Do not claim that prioritising decision rights is novel, or that decision rights alone ensure accountability | Formal authority can coexist with automation bias, deskilling, weak intervention capacity and symbolic oversight | Recast if the five-dimension alignment model proves derivative | REFRAME — absorb into broader alignment framework |
| Governance as Permission | **Gardner book-level synthesis — approved for manuscript use** | Adler & Borys enabling versus coercive formalisation; Ahrens & Chapman; Wouters & Wilderom; Chapman & Kihn; Weill & Ross decision rights; NIST/OECD/Australian proportional risk governance; high-reliability organising; responsible innovation; red-tape and administrative-burden research | Integrate established enabling-control and risk-governance ideas into a reader-facing AI-governance lens connecting explicit boundaries, authority, evidence, conditional approval, exceptions, escalation and revocation | “Governance as Permission is an organisational lens for designing governance so that people and systems can act within explicit, proportionate and reviewable boundaries, with defined authority, evidence requirements, exception paths, escalation and the ability to restrict or withdraw permission when conditions change.” Supporting propositions: “Well-designed governance creates legitimate room to act”; “The opposite of weak governance is not necessarily more governance. It is better-designed governance”; “Permission to act is not permission to ignore risk.” | Do not claim first theory, empirical validation, legal doctrine, certification, universal scoring model, or automatic increases in speed, trust, innovation, ROI or adoption; do not equate permission with reduced control or power to ignore law, rights, safety or ethics | Enabling controls may become coercive in hierarchical use; clarity can preserve the wrong boundary; proportionality can be gamed; exceptions can normalise erosion; too little process can increase hesitation; too much can drive workarounds; responsible innovation has institutional limits | If substantial prior art duplicates the complete integration; if the chapter treats permission as deregulation; if evidence fails to distinguish enabling design from weak control; if manuscript claims automatic performance outcomes | APPROVED — retain as Gardner integration with explicit prior-art, non-validation and institutional-accountability boundaries |
| Authority–Judgement–Accountability Alignment | **Gardner synthesis — candidate core framework** | Flemisch et al. 2012 ability-authority-responsibility-control; Calvert et al. 2022 meaningful human control; Endsley & Kiris 1995 out-of-the-loop performance; Elish 2019 moral crumple zones; Zhu et al. 2026 meaningful oversight; Australian AI safety guidance | Existing work treats meaningful human control, capability, authority and responsibility strongly, but the book can synthesise these into an organisational governance test for AI-enabled work: does the accountable human retain sufficient capability, information, time, authority and intervention capacity to exercise judgement? | Accountability is meaningful only where organisational design preserves the practical conditions required to exercise judgement and control | Do not claim the relationship between ability, authority and responsibility is new; do not claim every AI decision requires synchronous human approval | Existing meaningful-human-control literature already contains much of the conceptual foundation; human review can also create bottlenecks or reduce performance if badly designed | If further provenance shows an existing organisational model already combines all five dimensions in substantially the same way | PROMISING — develop as synthesis, not invention |
| Accountability–Control Gap | **Gardner synthesis / book-level diagnostic phrase — candidate** | Elish moral crumple zones; responsibility-gap literature; meaningful human control; out-of-the-loop research | Give executives a practical diagnostic for situations where formal accountability exceeds the real capacity to understand, intervene or control | Use to describe a mismatch between assigned accountability and practical control/capability | Do not present the underlying phenomenon as newly discovered | “Gap” may be too broad unless operationalised; distributed accountability may be legitimate | If it cannot be measured or diagnosed consistently | DEVELOP — possible reader-facing diagnostic |
| Capability as Governance | **Gardner book-level synthesis — approved for manuscript use** | AI literacy; training-transfer research; calibrated trust and appropriate reliance; automation bias; expertise development; organisational AI readiness; psychological safety; employee voice | Existing fields explain separate components, but the book integrates them into one governance argument: capability is part of the control system because meaningful accountability depends on people being able to recognise, judge, challenge, escalate and intervene in context | “Capability is not merely preparation for AI governance. It is one of the mechanisms through which governance operates.” Supporting line: “Compliance can show that training occurred. Capability asks whether judgement can still occur when it matters.” | Do not claim a new competency framework, validated capability scale, permanent certification, universal deskilling effect, or that training and compliance evidence are useless | AI can augment performance and spread expert practice; training can work; psychological safety is insufficient alone; capability may be collective and context-specific | If stronger evidence shows the synthesis cannot be distinguished from an existing organisational model, or if the manuscript drifts into a training framework | APPROVED — retain as Gardner integration with non-framework and non-certification boundaries |
