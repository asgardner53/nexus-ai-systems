## Approved chapter argument

The chapter establishes that human and organisational capability is part of the AI governance system itself. Training, policy acknowledgement and literacy remain important, but they do not alone establish that a person or accountable arrangement can recognise a material problem, interpret the relevant evidence, challenge an AI-shaped recommendation and use intervention or escalation pathways in practice.

Approved governing proposition:

> **Capability is not merely preparation for AI governance. It is one of the mechanisms through which governance operates.**

Approved supporting proposition:

> **Compliance can show that training occurred. Capability asks whether judgement can still occur when it matters.**

## Approved intellectual boundaries

The chapter distinguishes among:

- AI literacy;
- formal training;
- learning transfer;
- domain and task judgement;
- calibrated reliance;
- expertise development and retention;
- manager and accountable-role capability;
- collective capability;
- psychological permission to challenge;
- contextual evidence of operational capability.

The chapter does not present a new competency framework, maturity ladder, validated assessment instrument, professional credential or compliance certificate.

It does not claim that:

- AI inevitably deskills workers;
- literacy or training automatically improves decisions;
- explanations or confidence displays guarantee appropriate reliance;
- human–AI combinations automatically outperform humans or AI operating alone;
- psychological safety alone guarantees effective voice;
- every individual must possess every specialised capability;
- one assessment can certify permanent AI-governance competence.
