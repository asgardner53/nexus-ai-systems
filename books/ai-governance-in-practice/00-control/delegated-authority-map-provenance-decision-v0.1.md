## Proposed construct

**Name:** Delegated Authority Map

**Recommended definition:**

> **The Delegated Authority Map is a practical organisational diagnostic that identifies the functions an AI system may perform, the actions it may take without fresh human approval, the boundaries within which that permission applies, the controls that govern it, the recourse available when conditions change, and the human or institutional accountability that remains.**

## Recommended structure

### Function

What part of the work or decision may the AI perform?

### Permission

What decisions or actions may it undertake without obtaining fresh human approval?

### Boundary

Within what purpose, context, population, data, tools, resources, transaction, time and risk limits does that permission apply?

### Control

What approval, supervision, monitoring, evidence, access, testing and change-control arrangements govern the authority while active?

### Recourse

When must the system defer, escalate, pause, permit override, roll back, accept restriction, lose permission or be decommissioned?

### Accountability overlay

Who authorised the delegation, owns its outcomes and learning, monitors its use, can modify or revoke it, and remains responsible for conditions controlled elsewhere?

## Governing proposition

> **Capability does not confer authority.**

Supporting proposition:

> **Delegation does not transfer accountability.**

## Evidence basis

The map is informed by established work on:

- types and levels of automation;
- decision functions and task allocation;
- adaptive and variable autonomy;
- human–autonomy teaming;
- ability, authority, responsibility and control;
- meaningful human control;
- risk- and ambiguity-based decision rights;
- accountability configuration;
- operating domains, guardrails and permissions;
- monitoring, override, rollback, revocation and decommissioning.

## Material prior art

Material neighbouring work includes:

- Parasuraman, Sheridan and Wickens' types and levels of automation;
- Endsley and Kaber's empirical work on automation levels;
- Miller and Parasuraman's delegation interfaces;
- Flemisch et al.'s ability–authority–responsibility–control relationship;
- meaningful-human-control and variable-autonomy frameworks;
- MIT CISR's risk/ambiguity and frame–act–learn decision allocation;
- Engin's Human-AI Governance configuration;
- contemporary agent-autonomy and capability-versus-permission frameworks.

The components are not newly discovered by Alec Gardner.

## Defensible original contribution

The Gardner contribution is:

1. making **delegated organisational authority** the executive governance question;
2. distinguishing system capability from organisational permission;
3. replacing a single autonomy ladder with a function-specific authority map;
4. integrating function, permission, boundary, control, recourse and accountability;
5. connecting the map directly to the Accountability–Control Gap and Meaningful Accountability Test;
6. creating a bridge between governance intent and the technical/workflow infrastructure developed in Chapter 6.

## Claims prohibited

Do not state or imply that the map is:

- the first framework for allocating AI authority;
- a novel discovery of capability-versus-permission distinctions;
- a validated scientific scale;
- a legal test or transfer of legal authority to AI;
- a compliance certificate;
- a universal autonomy taxonomy;
- a numerical maturity score;
- proof that a mapped use is safe, fair, lawful or appropriate.

## Terminology boundary

**Authority** means organisational permission to make or execute defined decisions or actions within a controlled operating arrangement.

It does not mean that the AI possesses:

- legal personhood;
- moral agency;
- independent institutional accountability;
- unrestricted discretion;
- authority outside the permissions and boundaries established by the organisation.

## Design decision

The map should not be presented as a vertical ladder. A ladder implies one direction and one overall level. In practice, an AI system may have different authority across information, recommendation, decision, execution and adaptation functions.

The preferred visual is therefore a structured map or table showing:

- functions down one axis;
- permission and boundary conditions across the other;
- control and recourse attached to each material authority;
- named accountability across the complete arrangement.
