# Concept Provenance Register

| Concept | Origin/status | Neighbouring scholarship / prior art | Conceptual gap | Exact claim permitted now | Claims excluded now | Hostile evidence / challenge | Revision trigger | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI Governance Operating System | Gardner organising metaphor — **not original as a label** | ISO/IEC 42001 AI management system; NIST AI RMF; commercial AI-governance platforms and related governance-operating-system terminology | The contribution is the specific organisational integration linking strategy, decision authority, risk, data, controls, human judgement, capability, evidence and assurance | Use *operating system* as a disclosed metaphor for an integrated organisational governance model, differentiated from software products and management-system standards | Do not claim first use, proprietary naming, empirical validation or uniqueness of the phrase | The metaphor may be read as software-platform language or repackaged ISO management-system thinking | If the architecture does not add materially distinct organisational value | REFRAME — retain metaphor, abandon naming originality |
| Evolved Governance Chain | **Gardner book-level synthesis — approved for manuscript use** | IAASB assurance standards; PCAOB audit evidence; COSO; IIA; ISO/IEC 42001, 42006 and ISO 19011; NIST; UK and Australian assurance guidance; EU AI Act; algorithmic auditing, decision provenance, reviewability and continuous auditing | Integrate Meaningful Accountability into a traceability spine linking purpose, authorised authority, boundary, execution, evidence, evaluation and learning while treating accountability, risk, judgement, controls, monitoring and change as cross-cutting | “The Evolved Governance Chain is a diagnostic traceability spine linking Purpose → Authority → Boundary → Execution → Evidence → Evaluation → Learning. Assurance is the conclusion produced by evaluating a defined governance claim and the relevant spine against suitable criteria and sufficient, appropriate evidence.” | Do not claim a universal lifecycle, causal law, mandatory sequence, maturity model, audit standard, certification method, external validation, complete reconstructability or proof that a use is safe, fair, lawful, trustworthy or effective | “Chain” may imply false linearity; existing cycles provide substantial prior art; a complete record can preserve a poor decision; auditability can privilege what is measurable | If assurance becomes a chain link, cross-cutting conditions become sequential boxes, or the construct adds no diagnostic value | APPROVED — retain as a non-linear diagnostic traceability spine with explicit prior-art and non-validation boundaries |
| Decision Rights Before Controls | **Established neighbouring idea; Gardner phrasing not distinctive** | Weill and Ross IT governance; MIT CISR decision-rights work; contemporary AI-accountability frameworks | Stronger contribution lies in aligning authority, capability, evidence access, intervention capacity and accountability | State that explicit decision rights are a foundational governance requirement | Do not claim that prioritising decision rights is novel or sufficient for accountability | Formal authority can coexist with automation bias, weak intervention capacity and symbolic oversight | Recast if the wider alignment model proves derivative | REFRAME — absorb into broader alignment framework |
| Governance as Permission | **Gardner book-level synthesis — approved for manuscript use** | Enabling and coercive formalisation; management-control research; decision rights; NIST/OECD/Australian proportional risk governance; high-reliability organising; responsible innovation; administrative-burden research | Integrate established enabling-control and risk-governance ideas into a reader-facing AI-governance lens connecting boundaries, authority, evidence, conditional approval, exceptions, escalation and revocation | “Governance as Permission is an organisational lens for designing governance so that people and systems can act within explicit, proportionate and reviewable boundaries, with defined authority, evidence requirements, exception paths, escalation and the ability to restrict or withdraw permission when conditions change.” | Do not claim first theory, empirical validation, legal doctrine, certification, universal scoring or automatic increases in speed, trust, innovation, ROI or adoption | Enabling controls may become coercive; clarity can preserve the wrong boundary; proportionality can be gamed; exceptions can normalise erosion | If prior art duplicates the integration or the chapter drifts into deregulation or automatic performance claims | APPROVED — retain as Gardner integration with explicit prior-art and institutional-accountability boundaries |
| Authority–Judgement–Accountability Alignment | **Gardner synthesis — candidate core framework** | Ability–authority–responsibility–control research; meaningful human control; out-of-the-loop research; moral crumple zones; Australian AI guidance | Synthesize whether accountable humans retain capability, information, time, authority and intervention capacity | Accountability is meaningful only where organisational design preserves the practical conditions required to exercise judgement and control | Do not claim the relationship among ability, authority and responsibility is new or that every AI decision requires synchronous human approval | Existing meaningful-human-control literature contains much of the conceptual foundation; badly designed human review can create bottlenecks | If an existing model already combines the dimensions substantially as proposed | PROMISING — develop as synthesis, not invention |
| Accountability–Control Gap | **Gardner synthesis / book-level diagnostic phrase — candidate** | Moral crumple zones; responsibility-gap literature; meaningful human control; out-of-the-loop research | Give executives a practical diagnostic for formal accountability exceeding practical capacity to understand, intervene or control | Use to describe a mismatch between assigned accountability and practical control or capability | Do not present the underlying phenomenon as newly discovered | The phrase may be too broad without operationalisation; distributed accountability may be legitimate | If it cannot be diagnosed consistently | DEVELOP — possible reader-facing diagnostic |
| Capability as Governance | **Gardner book-level synthesis — approved for manuscript use** | AI literacy; training transfer; calibrated reliance; automation bias; expertise development; organisational readiness; psychological safety; employee voice | Integrate separate fields into one governance argument: capability is part of control because accountability depends on recognising, judging, challenging, escalating and intervening in context | “Capability is not merely preparation for AI governance. It is one of the mechanisms through which governance operates.” Supporting line: “Compliance can show that training occurred. Capability asks whether judgement can still occur when it matters.” | Do not claim a new competency framework, validated scale, permanent certification, universal deskilling effect or that training and compliance evidence are useless | AI can augment performance; training can work; psychological safety is insufficient; capability may be collective and context-specific | If the synthesis cannot be distinguished from an existing model or drifts into a training framework | APPROVED — retain as Gardner integration with non-framework and non-certification boundaries |
| Chapter 10 runtime-action synthesis | **Gardner book-level synthesis — approved; no new named framework** | NIST agent identity and security; allied cyber guidance; OECD agentic definitions; Australian AI Safety Institute multi-agent work; A2A and MCP; OWASP agent controls; runtime assurance; meaningful human control; decision provenance; agent-security benchmarks | Integrate the book's accountability, delegated-authority, infrastructure, capability, permission and assurance constructs at the point delegated permission becomes action | “When AI can act, governance must be enforceable at the point where authority becomes action.” “Delegated action should remain bounded, attributable, observable, interruptible and revocable, with reversibility or defined recourse proportionate to consequence.” “The relevant governance object is the complete action path connecting identity, authority, tools, state changes, counterparties, evidence and recourse — not the model or final output alone.” | Do not claim a new universal framework, runtime-governance theory, authority-envelope invention, technical standard, maturity model, certification method, complete observability, universal human approval or guaranteed safe intervention | Risks are partly evolutionary; approval can become ceremonial; runtime controls can encode bad policy; traces may be incomplete; kill switches can cause harm; protocols do not confer legitimate authority; benchmarks have transfer limits | If material prior art duplicates the complete integration, prose treats descriptive terms as Gardner constructs, or a new framework is introduced unnecessarily | APPROVED — whole-book integration only; no new named Chapter 10 framework |
