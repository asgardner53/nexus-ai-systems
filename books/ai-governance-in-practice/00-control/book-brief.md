## Central tension

As AI becomes more embedded in work, humans can remain formally accountable while practical understanding, decision authority and control progressively migrate into systems, workflows, vendors and automated actions.

## Central argument

AI governance is the organisational discipline of keeping authority, human judgement, evidence, control and accountability aligned as AI moves from assisting work to acting within it.

The operating-system metaphor remains useful, but is not claimed as an original label. The book's original contribution must reside in its organisational synthesis and practical governance architecture.

## Primary reader

Executives, directors, managers, HR and workforce leaders, risk and governance professionals, transformation leaders and people responsible for introducing AI into organisations.

## Reader promise

The book should move the reader from asking:

> What AI policy do we need?

towards asking:

> What organisational conditions must exist for accountability to remain meaningful as AI gains greater influence and authority?

## Governing question

> What happens when a person remains accountable for a decision they are progressively less able to understand, challenge or control?

## Core working propositions

1. **Formal accountability becomes fragile when the person held responsible lacks the capability, visibility, authority or practical means to exercise meaningful control.**
2. **Accountability should not outrun capability, visibility, authority or intervention capacity.**
3. **Human approval is not the same as human control.**
4. **Human presence is not the same as human agency.**
5. **Well-designed governance can create conditions for confident action by clarifying boundaries, authority and escalation.**
6. **As AI moves from recommendation to action, governance must address delegated organisational authority, not only output review.**

## Working original frameworks

- **Meaningful Accountability Test** — Gardner synthesis informed by meaningful-human-control, human-factors and accountability scholarship.
- **Accountability–Control Gap** — reader-facing diagnostic phrase for misalignment between assigned responsibility and practical control.
- **Delegated Authority Map** — to be developed after further evidence mapping.
- **Governance as Permission** — central Gardner hypothesis, subject to continued evidence qualification.
- **Evolved Governance Chain** — purpose/use → delegated authority → risk/exposure → required human judgement → controls → accountable owner → evidence → monitoring → intervention/learning.
