# Chapter 7 v0.1 — Forensic Evidence Survival, Citation Visibility and Integrity Review

**Chapter:** Capability Before Compliance  
**Date:** 2026-11-18  
**Materiality:** M3  
**Decision:** **CONDITIONAL PASS — targeted Author Revision required**

## Overall judgement

The chapter's evidence spine survived drafting. Material claims remain visible, properly bounded and connected to authoritative or academic evidence.

The manuscript preserves both sides of the evidence:

- AI can strengthen performance and spread aspects of expert practice;
- AI can also change cognitive effort, reduce scrutiny or weaken opportunities to develop and maintain judgement;
- training can build capability;
- training completion alone does not prove transfer into work;
- psychological safety supports voice;
- psychological safety does not guarantee detection, challenge or action;
- human-AI combinations can improve human performance;
- they do not automatically outperform the better of human or AI alone.

No invented source, retracted source, unsupported statistic or false novelty claim was identified.

Five local repairs are required before Evidence PASS. None requires a full rewrite.

## Claim-by-claim survival

### C07-001 — Capability as a governance mechanism

**Outcome: PASS**

The dominant proposition is clearly presented as Gardner organisational synthesis rather than an external definition.

The manuscript supports it through the EU AI Act, NIST and Australian guidance without claiming that those sources use the same formulation.

### C07-002 and C07-003 — AI literacy and operational competence

**Outcome: PASS**

Long and Magerko and Ng et al. remain visible at the point where the chapter broadens AI literacy beyond tool use.

The manuscript correctly distinguishes literacy from role-specific domain judgement and intervention capability.

No claim is made that literacy training is ineffective.

### C07-004 — Training transfer

**Outcome: PASS**

Baldwin and Ford and Grossman and Salas visibly support the distinction between learning and generalisation/maintenance in work.

The manuscript retains the corrective boundary that effective training can materially improve capability.

### C07-005 to C07-007 — Calibrated reliance, automation bias and explanations

**Outcome: PASS**

The chapter visibly retains:

- appropriate reliance rather than maximum trust;
- both over-reliance and under-reliance;
- context-dependent automation bias;
- the limits of explanations and confidence displays;
- the effort and performance trade-offs of cognitive forcing.

The wording remains bounded and does not claim universal effects.

### C07-008 — Human-AI performance

**Outcome: PASS WITH PRECISION REPAIR**

The key meta-analytic finding survives accurately: the combination improved human performance on average but did not outperform the better of human or AI alone on average.

The manuscript should add that this conclusion applies to the performance dimensions and experiments included in the review, preventing a universal claim about all human-AI collaboration.

**Repair ID: E7-01**

### C07-009 — Workplace augmentation

**Outcome: PASS WITH PRECISION REPAIR**

The Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond finding is accurately represented. Add the study's productivity measure — issues resolved per hour — so the 15 per cent result is not read as a universal productivity construct.

**Repair ID: E7-02**

### C07-010 — Critical-thinking effort

**Outcome: PASS**

The manuscript preserves the association, sample size, example count and stated limitations. It does not claim causal cognitive decline.

### C07-011 — Expertise development and retention

**Outcome: PASS**

The manuscript presents augmentation, cognitive redistribution and capability erosion as alternative design outcomes.

It does not state that AI inevitably deskills people.

### C07-012 — Manager capability

**Outcome: CONDITIONAL PASS**

The manager-capability passage is materially important and currently supported by authoritative sources earlier in the chapter. Add a local citation to NIST and Australian guidance where the role-specific management responsibilities are developed, ensuring citation visibility survives extraction or later editing.

**Repair ID: E7-03**

### C07-013 — Distributed capability

**Outcome: CONDITIONAL PASS**

The argument that capability may reside across a team or governance arrangement is defensible and consistent with NIST's emphasis on diverse expertise.

Add a proportionate NIST citation at the collective-capability passage. Preserve the condition that expertise must be assembled reliably and in time.

**Repair ID: E7-04**

### C07-014 and C07-015 — Psychological safety, voice and challenge

**Outcome: PASS**

Edmondson, Detert and Burris, Morrison, and O'Donovan and McAuliffe visibly support the voice and psychological-safety movement.

The chapter clearly states that psychological safety is not sufficient.

### C07-016 — Workplace AI-training content

**Outcome: PASS**

The chapter does not overuse Zhang et al. (2025), but the research remains available in the evidence register. The manuscript's main argument does not depend on universalising the qualitative findings.

### C07-017 — Evidence of capability

**Outcome: CONDITIONAL PASS**

The manuscript says that no validated organisational measure currently proves AI-governance capability across every role and system. This is directionally consistent with the research review but is too categorical for publication without a formal systematic measurement review.

Narrow the sentence to:

> No widely established, context-neutral measure can demonstrate AI-governance capability across every role and system.

This preserves the practical boundary without claiming exhaustive absence.

**Repair ID: E7-05**

### C07-018 — Compliance and capability distinction

**Outcome: PASS**

The proposition is properly framed as Gardner synthesis. The manuscript repeatedly preserves the value of compliance evidence and formal training.

The chapter introduces no new named construct.

- **Capability is not merely preparation for AI governance. It is one of the mechanisms through which governance operates.**
