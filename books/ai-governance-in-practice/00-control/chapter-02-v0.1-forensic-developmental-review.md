# Chapter 2 v0.1 — Forensic Evidence Survival + Developmental Review

**Chapter:** When Policy Exists but Control Does Not  
**Date:** 2026-11-01  
**Overall decision:** **SUBSTANTIAL REVISION REQUIRED**

## Overall judgement

The chapter has a strong central argument, a clean role in the book architecture and an effective transition into Chapter 3. The key distinction — policy as declared intent versus governance as enacted control — is defensible and useful.

However, the chapter cannot yet receive developmental approval for two reasons:

1. **Gate 3 Evidence FAIL:** material evidence claims from the research pack have not survived into the manuscript with sufficient citation visibility.
2. **Gate 5 Book Prose FAIL:** the prose still contains too many short rhetorical paragraphs, mini-conclusions and article-style emphatic breaks.

The argument should be preserved. The next version should primarily repair evidentiary traceability and paragraph movement.

## Gate 3 — Evidence

**Outcome: FAIL**

### Evidence that survives correctly

- ISO/IEC 42001 is accurately represented as an AI management-system standard requiring policies, objectives, processes, implementation, performance evaluation and continual improvement.
- NIST AI RMF Govern is accurately represented as requiring policies, processes, procedures and practices to be effectively implemented, with accountability structures, roles, training, monitoring and review.
- The chapter appropriately treats policy as necessary rather than dismissing it.
- Stanford AI Index 2026 is accurately characterised as showing increased responsible-AI formalisation alongside continuing implementation barriers.
- The chapter preserves the red-team boundary that weak implementation does not automatically equal “governance theatre”.

### Evidence survival failures

#### E2-01 — Decoupling scholarship is uncited in the manuscript

The chapter states:

> “Organisational researchers have long examined what happens when formal structures become separated from day-to-day practice.”

and introduces **decoupling** as an established organisational phenomenon.

The evidence pack contains appropriate scholarship, but the controlled manuscript provides no in-text citation to Westphal & Zajac or related decoupling literature.

**Required repair:** add light Harvard-style citation at first introduction of decoupling.

#### E2-02 — Current AI implementation research is uncited

The manuscript states:

> “Studies of developers and governance practitioners report recurring difficulties in translating high-level commitments into usable processes...”

This is a material empirical claim and currently lacks traceable citation.

**Required repair:** cite the relevant 2025 implementation studies in the prose.

#### E2-03 — Public-organisation internalisation claim is unsupported on-page

The manuscript states:

> “Organisations that successfully internalise strategy, policy, standards, training and clear responsibilities can build stronger governance capability.”

This is reasonable and supported in the evidence pack, but the manuscript does not identify the supporting study.

**Required repair:** cite the relevant public-organisation governance study, or soften to a synthesis grounded explicitly in NIST/ISO.

### Evidence decision

Because several material claims rely on research that is present only in the evidence pack rather than the manuscript, Gate 3 fails the Citation Visibility Test.

This is a manuscript-survival failure, not a research-quality failure.

## Gate 4 — Narrative

**Outcome: CONDITIONAL PASS**

### Strengths
- The opening contradiction is strong: respectable governance architecture on paper, uncertainty in live decisions.
- The chapter does not caricature policy.
- The transition from decoupling theory into AI practice is coherent.
- The final question leads naturally into Chapter 3.

### Weakness
The chapter explains its lesson too frequently immediately after demonstrating it. The narrative repeatedly pauses for statements such as:

- “The policy has not failed because it exists. It has failed to become coupled to practice.”
- “The language matters because it changes the unit of attention.”
- “None of those mechanisms is glamorous. They are also where governance becomes real.”
- “The problem is not that these artefacts are unnecessary. It is that they acquire meaning only through their connection to the work.”
- “Those harder problems are where the work lies.”

Several are individually effective; together they reduce intellectual delay.

**Required repair:** retain a small number of these turns and absorb the rest into sustained paragraphs.

## Gate 5 — Book-Prose Continuity

**Outcome: FAIL**

The chapter retains visible article/newsletter cadence.

Presumptive defects include:
- consecutive one-sentence paragraphs;
- repeated rhetorical question clusters;
- mini-conclusion paragraphs;
- proposition → punch line → proposition rhythm;
- isolated aphoristic lines near the close.

Examples:
- “From a distance, it looks reassuring.”
- “Then someone asks a practical question.”
- “The policy has not failed because it exists. It has failed to become coupled to practice.”
- “None of those mechanisms is glamorous. They are also where governance becomes real.”
- “Those harder problems are where the work lies.”
- “A policy can remain unchanged while the risk around it changes completely.”
- “Policy tells the organisation what it intends. Governance determines what actually happens.”
- “But that raises a more difficult question.”

The chapter reads as strong executive thought leadership, but not yet consistently as sustained book prose.

**Required repair:** rebuild these sections as paragraph movements rather than mechanically joining lines.

## Gate 6 — Theory & Authenticity

**Outcome: PASS**

The theory is well controlled.

- Decoupling is correctly treated as established organisational scholarship, not Gardner invention.
- “Governance exists where principles alter decisions, permissions, controls, evidence and behaviour” is clearly Gardner synthesis.
- “Policy tells the organisation what it intends. Governance determines what actually happens” is a framing proposition rather than a claimed external definition.
- The chapter avoids overclaiming “governance theatre”.
- Formalisation is treated as potentially substantive when coupled to practice.

No provenance defect requires theoretical rework.

## Gate 7 — Reader Engrossment

**Outcome: CONDITIONAL PASS**

The opening and ending are strong and the chapter contains a useful tension. The middle becomes somewhat didactic because the reader is repeatedly told the meaning of each movement.

The live-case example helps, but the chapter would be more engrossing if it stayed inside the operational uncertainty longer before resolving it.

## Whole-book flow

**Outcome: PASS**

The chapter performs a distinct job:
- Chapter 1 asks where AI is participating in work.
- Chapter 2 asks whether governance artefacts actually control that work.
- Chapter 3 can now ask whether the human who remains accountable still possesses meaningful control.

The chapter does not prematurely introduce the Meaningful Accountability Test.

## Priority revisions

1. **Restore manuscript-level citations** for decoupling scholarship and current AI implementation evidence.
2. **Rebuild short rhetorical paragraphs** into sustained book prose.
3. **Reduce mini-conclusions** and allow the reader to infer more.
4. **Tighten the middle third**, particularly the sequence on artefacts, committee, form and register.
5. Preserve the final accountability question.
