# Forensic Research Brief 01 — Core Construct Provenance

**Date:** 2026-11-01  
**Materiality:** M3 — publication theory and originality control  
**Status:** For Alec Gardner editorial decision

## Research question

Are the four proposed core constructs for *AI Governance in Practice* sufficiently distinctive and evidence-grounded to support the book’s central argument?

## Findings

### 1. AI Governance Operating System

**Finding:** the phrase is not original and should not be presented as such.

ISO/IEC 42001 already frames AI governance through an organisational management system. In 2025–26 multiple commercial platforms explicitly market themselves as an “AI Governance Operating System”, and a 2026 research series also uses “Governance Operating System” terminology.

**Decision implication:** retain **operating system** only as an organising metaphor. The book’s originality must reside in the architecture it builds: the relationship among strategy, decision authority, human judgement, risk, data, controls, capability, evidence, monitoring and assurance.

### 2. Decision Rights Before Controls

**Finding:** decision rights are established governance scholarship rather than a new Gardner construct.

Weill and Ross established decision rights and accountability as central to IT governance. MIT CISR’s 2026 research now applies this directly to AI, including how humans and AI share framing, action and learning according to ambiguity and risk.

**Decision implication:** abandon any novelty claim around prioritising decision rights. Develop the stronger proposition that **formal decision rights are insufficient unless authority, competence, evidence access, intervention capacity and accountability remain aligned**.

### 3. Governance as Permission

**Finding:** this is promising but sits beside established enabling-control theory.

Adler and Borys’ enabling-versus-coercive formalisation shows that formal systems can either support people in performing work or become mechanisms of constraint. Weill and Ross likewise frame governance as both empowerment and control. Contemporary responsible-AI sources increasingly argue that assurance and governance can support confidence and scaled adoption.

**Decision implication:** retain this as a central Gardner hypothesis, but phrase it carefully: **well-designed governance can create conditions for confident action**. Do not claim that governance inherently causes speed, innovation, ROI or trust.

### 4. Governance Chain

**Finding:** no exact equivalent has been established in this first pass, but its components overlap heavily with NIST, ISO management-system cycles, risk/control models and assurance practice.

**Decision implication:** continue provenance work. Its possible value is not the individual stages but the traceability of a real AI use from purpose and risk through accountable ownership, evidence, monitoring and improvement.

## Strongest emerging original territory

The research suggests the book should move away from claiming novelty in labels and move toward a more defensible synthesis:

> **AI governance is the organisational design problem of keeping authority, human judgement, evidence, control and accountability aligned as AI moves from assisting work to acting within it.**

A possible distinctive framework should therefore connect:

**purpose/use → delegated authority → risk/exposure → required human judgement → controls → accountable owner → evidence → monitoring → intervention/learning**

This is stronger than “governance operating system” as a branded phrase and more distinctive than decision rights alone.

## Red-team challenge

The book must confront four risks in its own thesis:

1. More governance can create delay, bureaucracy and false assurance.
2. Decision rights can be formally clear while human judgement capacity quietly erodes.
3. Human oversight can become symbolic when reviewers cannot understand, contest or practically override AI outputs.
4. “Operating system” language can imply technical centralisation when effective governance may be distributed and adaptive.

## Safe claims now

- AI governance can be treated as an integrated organisational system rather than merely a policy layer.
- Explicit decision rights and accountability are established components of effective technology/AI governance.
- Human presence does not by itself guarantee meaningful oversight.
- Governance can be enabling or constraining depending on design and context.
- Agentic systems increase the importance of defining what authority is delegated to AI and under what conditions.

## Claims requiring continued verification

- Good AI governance measurably accelerates adoption.
- Good AI governance improves ROI, innovation or organisational performance.
- The proposed Governance Chain is novel enough to name as a proprietary Gardner model.
- Any claim that the proposed integrated framework is the first or uniquely comprehensive model.
