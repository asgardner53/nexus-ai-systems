## Purpose

This standard governs all Nexus BMG AI-enabled Studios, including AIRBOK, assessment, publishing, masterclass, AI Ready and internal workflow Studios.

**Core principle: AI assists. Humans decide.**

## Governance principles

1. Every Studio must have a defined purpose.
2. A named human remains accountable for outcomes.
3. Controls must be proportionate to risk.
4. AI confidence is not evidence.
5. Access must be limited to the minimum necessary.
6. Users must understand the Studio's purpose, sources, actions and limits.
7. Material changes must be documented and reviewed.

## Studio classifications

- **Assistive:** low-risk brainstorming, formatting and simple drafting.
- **Professional:** substantive professional drafting, research and documentation.
- **Controlled:** work affecting governance, compliance, organisational risk or public credibility.
- **Restricted:** work involving sensitive information, formal determinations or significant consequences.

The Assessment Review Studio is Restricted. The AIRBOK Development Studio is Controlled.

## Model and reasoning selection

Use capability proportionate to the task:

- efficient mode for routine rewriting, formatting and extraction;
- standard reasoning for professional drafting and ordinary analysis;
- extended reasoning for complex evidence review, governance work and multi-document analysis;
- maximum reasoning or multi-agent workflows only for genuinely complex, high-value or high-risk work.

Studio instructions should describe the required capability rather than depend permanently on a specific model name.

## Agent authority

AI may organise information, identify gaps, summarise sources, compare evidence, draft materials, conduct authorised research and prepare recommendations.

Human approval is required before external communication, publication, controlled-record changes, regulatory submission, formal decisions, competency outcomes, material professional advice or presentation of doctrine as approved.

AI must not autonomously make employment decisions, determine student competency, approve AIRBOK doctrine, disclose confidential information, invent evidence or bypass review.

## Source hierarchy

1. Controlled source documents.
2. Authoritative external evidence.
3. Approved internal guidance.
4. Current project context.
5. General model knowledge and memory.

Higher-level sources override lower-level sources. Conflicts must be identified and escalated.

## Evidence and citation

Material factual claims must be verifiable. Studios must distinguish evidence, interpretation and doctrine; use current authoritative sources; identify incomplete evidence; and preserve source records for Controlled and Restricted work.

AIRBOK must distinguish external evidence, author interpretation, emerging doctrine and formally approved doctrine.

## Memory

Memory may support preferences and continuity but is not the formal source of truth for approvals, assessment evidence, competency outcomes, doctrine status, manuscript status, controlled terminology or governance decisions.

## Plugins and connectors

Every connection must have an approved purpose, defined data access, identified write authority, authorised users, human review points, risk controls and a review date.

## Privacy

Personal, confidential and commercially sensitive information must be minimised, de-identified where practical, restricted to authorised users and excluded from public outputs.

## Quality gates

Every material output must pass checks for completeness, evidence, accuracy, reasoning, risk, privacy, human judgement, authority, style and recordkeeping.

## Cost and usage

Use efficient capability for routine work and extended capability only where complexity or risk warrants it. Avoid unnecessary whole-document regeneration and duplicated agent activity.

## Escalation

Stop and escalate where evidence is missing, instructions conflict, authority is unclear, privacy may be compromised, the action exceeds permissions, a person may be materially affected, a critical claim cannot be verified or formal approval is required.

## Studio control records

Every Studio must maintain a control record stating its purpose, owner, classification, users, approved sources, connectors, permitted actions, approval points, prohibited actions, quality gates, version, review date and status.

## GitHub control

All controlled governance documents, templates, registers and Studio control records must be stored and versioned in the approved GitHub repository under the GitHub Document Control Standard.
