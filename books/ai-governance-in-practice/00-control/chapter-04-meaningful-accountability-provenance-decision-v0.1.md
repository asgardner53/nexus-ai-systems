## Decision

Retain the name **Meaningful Accountability Test** and retain the five proposed conditions:

1. Capability
2. Visibility
3. Authority
4. Intervention capacity
5. Accountability alignment

The test is approved for manuscript development as **Gardner organisational synthesis / emerging doctrine** under the following provenance controls.

## Approved provenance position

1. The five component ideas are not claimed as newly discovered.
2. The manuscript will acknowledge that meaningful-human-control, effective-oversight, human-factors, legal and regulatory scholarship provides substantial prior foundations.
3. Material close prior art includes Flemisch et al., Siebert et al., Sterz et al., Zhu et al., Nitchals, and van de Sande et al.
4. The defensible Gardner contribution is:
   - concise organisational packaging;
   - use of accountability as the organising question;
   - linkage to the Accountability–Control Gap;
   - application to a person, role, team or organisational arrangement;
   - integration into the book's governance operating system;
   - the practical proposition that accountability should not outrun capability, visibility, authority or intervention capacity.
5. The test will be described as a **practical organisational diagnostic**, not as:
   - a validated scientific instrument;
   - a universal legal test;
   - a compliance certification;
   - a numerical maturity scale;
   - the first framework of its type.
6. Passing the diagnostic does not itself establish that an AI use is safe, ethical, compliant or suitable to deploy.
7. Alec Gardner retains final approval of the wording, interpretation, doctrine transfer and publication use.

## Approved controlled definition

> **The Meaningful Accountability Test is a practical organisational diagnostic for examining whether a person, role or organisation assigned accountability for an AI-mediated decision has the capability, visibility, authority and intervention capacity needed to exercise that accountability in practice, and whether the responsibility assigned is proportionate to the control that can realistically be exercised.**

## Framework-use control

The five conditions will be presented as interdependent questions requiring context-specific judgement. They will not be converted into a mechanical score or universal threshold.

A material weakness in one condition may make accountability fragile even where other conditions appear strong, but the significance of the weakness must be assessed against consequence, uncertainty, autonomy, speed, reversibility, alternatives, organisational context and jurisdiction.
