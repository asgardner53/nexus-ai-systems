## Overall judgement

Chapter 6 v0.1 performs its required book-level function. It moves the manuscript from the delegated-authority decision established in Chapter 5 into the infrastructure through which that decision is enacted, observed and preserved.

The chapter does not become a catalogue of technical controls. Identity, data, workflow, documentation, logging, supplier arrangements and withdrawal remain parts of one organisational problem: the difference between a governance boundary that has been approved and one that is true in operation.

The composite supplier-onboarding case gives the argument a distinct narrative vehicle and avoids repeating Chapter 5's customer-service authority-drift case. The central proposition is earned after the reader has experienced the mismatch between the approval paper and the configured workflow.

No structural rewrite is required. Any v0.2 revision should be targeted to evidence visibility, provenance signalling and a small number of citation-entailment repairs. It should not widen the chapter or disturb its narrative architecture.

## Strongest elements

### The opening problem is ordinary and therefore credible

Nothing has visibly failed. The tension comes from a routine review discovering that the recommendation-only description no longer matches the service identity, workflow, data connection, logs or provider-update arrangement. This is more persuasive than a dramatic incident because it shows how governance changes through normal implementation decisions.

### The chapter preserves the distinction between governance and engineering

The manuscript repeatedly refuses two weak conclusions:

1. that policy alone governs the system; and
2. that technical control can decide whether the purpose or decision is legitimate.

The line that infrastructure should make the organisation's decision true in operation rather than make the decision for the organisation is particularly effective.

### The running case carries the conceptual domains

Access, data, workflow, documentation, provenance, supplier dependence and withdrawal all return to the supplier system. The reader therefore experiences one deepening organisational problem rather than seven standalone mini-essays.

### Contrary evidence survives

The chapter preserves important limits:

- least privilege does not establish legitimate purpose;
- complete provenance does not guarantee fairness or correctness;
- not every governance requirement should be hard-coded;
- documents can be accurate and later become stale;
- logs can be incomplete or excessive;
- contracts cannot eliminate supplier opacity;
- abrupt shutdown can create additional risk;
- infrastructure cannot supply judgement, courage or institutional willingness.

These qualifications strengthen rather than weaken the proposition.

### The ending creates forward pressure

The closing question — whether people carrying accountability can recognise when the control environment is no longer enough — leads naturally into Chapter 7, *Capability Before Compliance*, without summarising the chapter or announcing a training programme.

## Narrative review

### Reader entry belief

Governance decisions are made through policies, committees and approvals, after which technology implements them.

### What destabilises that belief

The service identity, automatic status change, post-action notification, wider data connection, partial log and provider update mechanism reveal that the operating arrangement has allocated authority differently from the approved description.

### Curiosity and discovery

The chapter delays the general proposition until after the reader has encountered the mismatch. Evidence then arrives where the running case creates a need for it: zero trust after the service-account discovery, data cascades after the data-source problem, reviewability after the workflow problem and decision provenance after the logging problem.

### Narrative risk checked

The sequence could easily have become access → data → workflow → logging → vendors as a visible controls article. It does not cross that line because the same supplier process remains the object under examination. During v0.2, citation repair must not create literature-review paragraphs or interrupt that continuity.

**Narrative outcome: PASS.**

## Book-prose review

The chapter contains a small number of short paragraphs, but each is narratively earned:

- the opening sentence;
- the central question;
- the governing proposition;
- the final question.

They do not form a repeated punch-line cadence. Most paragraphs develop a complete movement of scene, evidence, qualification or interpretation. There are no visible subheadings beyond the chapter title, no stacked quotations and no checklist presented as prose.

A few sentences contain internal enumerations, particularly around permissions, data attributes and control mechanisms. These lists remain analytical and proportionate. They should not be expanded during revision.

**Book-prose outcome: PASS.**

## Theory and originality review

The chapter does not claim originality for zero trust, access control, data governance, model cards, datasheets, FactSheets, decision provenance, internal audit, logging, configuration management or supply-chain governance.

The original contribution remains the book-level integration:

- connecting delegated authority to the infrastructure that makes its boundaries real;
- distinguishing governance intent, operational enactment and evidence of operating effectiveness;
- placing technical and organisational controls inside a wider institutional governance system;
- showing why the next problem is human capability rather than another control layer.

The central formulations must remain labelled in the evidence records as Gardner synthesis informed by established evidence.

**Theory and authenticity outcome: PASS WITH QUALIFICATION.**
