# Chapter 1 v0.2 — Platinum Gate Re-Review

**Chapter:** The AI You Are Already Governing  
**Date:** 2026-11-01  
**Decision:** **DEVELOPMENTAL APPROVAL — approved subject to light editing and final source verification**

## Gate 3 — Evidence

**Outcome: PASS**

The controlled manuscript retains traceable support for all consequential claims.

- Samsung is used narrowly and accurately as an illustration of unsanctioned generative-AI use creating information-governance risk.
- Australian Government register guidance supports capturing embedded AI features and use cases.
- Australian guidance directly supports the claim that the same AI system can carry materially different risks in different contexts of use.
- NIST supports context mapping beyond simple inventory.
- Australian screening guidance supports proportional governance.
- Cemex and Wipro remain clearly identified as vendor-authored implementation illustrations rather than independent effectiveness evidence.
- E1 is closed by changing the workarounds claim into a qualified organisational possibility.
- E2 is closed by removing the unsupported anchoring mechanism and retaining only the descriptive proposition that an AI-generated draft may become a starting point for later judgement.

The manuscript continues to distinguish evidence, interpretation and Gardner synthesis. No material source is being asked to support a stronger claim than it establishes.

## Gate 4 — Narrative

**Outcome: PASS**

The chapter now develops through discovery rather than repeated explanation.

The opening establishes a plausible organisational assumption: AI adoption begins with a deliberate project. Samsung disrupts that assumption through informal use. Cemex and Wipro then widen the problem by showing that AI can also become difficult to see because it is embedded in familiar infrastructure.

The reader reaches the inventory/use-case distinction after experiencing both forms of invisibility. Proportionality arrives as a complication rather than a checklist item, preventing the chapter from resolving into “catalogue everything”.

The ending does not summarise. It creates the next governance problem:
**what is governing what AI is allowed to do?**

## Gate 5 — Platinum Book-Prose Continuity

**Outcome: PASS**

The v0.1 defect has been materially repaired.

Paragraphs now generally carry complete intellectual movements. The manuscript no longer relies on serial one-line conclusions, stacked contrasts or newsletter-style page rhythm.

A small number of short paragraphs remain:
- “Increasingly, that moment does not exist.”
- “That is where the governance problem begins...”

These are scarce and narratively earned rather than habitual.

The transfer test is now satisfied: the chapter could not be lifted unchanged into a LinkedIn post without substantial restructuring.

## Gate 6 — Theory and Authenticity

**Outcome: PASS**

The chapter does not overclaim novelty.

- “Decision participation” is introduced as useful manuscript language, not as an empirically validated external construct.
- “Govern the work AI participates in, not merely the tools labelled AI” is visibly Gardner synthesis.
- The chapter does not claim inventories are ineffective; it reframes them as a starting point.
- The chapter preserves the boundary condition that not every AI use warrants bespoke governance.
- Vendor implementation examples are not converted into evidence of business value.

Red-team challenge remains represented through proportionality, generic controls and the warning against governance overload.

## Gate 7 — Reader Engrossment

**Outcome: PASS**

The chapter sustains curiosity through three successive shifts:

1. AI may enter work before formal adoption.
2. AI may also disappear into ordinary enterprise infrastructure.
3. Visibility alone is insufficient unless governance is proportionate to consequence.

The pacing is materially improved. The middle no longer feels like an evidence parade, and the chapter retains enough unresolved tension to make Chapter 2 necessary.
