## Three-Layer Evidence Survival Test

### Evidence establishes
- some automation arrangements can reduce situation awareness and make takeover more difficult;
- effective oversight for defined high-risk systems requires more than a named human role;
- accountability should reflect role, context and ability to act;
- responsibility can be misallocated to a nearby human with limited practical control;
- current human-oversight scholarship distinguishes AI operative agency from human evaluative agency.

### Reasonable interpretation
- a human sign-off does not by itself demonstrate meaningful control;
- accountability can remain formally assigned while practical control becomes thinner;
- distributed responsibility must remain legible rather than dissolve into shared involvement.

### Gardner synthesis
> **Delegation does not transfer accountability.**

> **Accountability–Control Gap:** the mismatch between the accountability assigned to a person, role or organisation and the practical control they can realistically exercise over the decision or outcome.

The manuscript does not imply that the cited sources externally validate either Gardner formulation.

## Contrary and boundary evidence retained

- automation does not inevitably deskill or weaken control in every context;
- more human touch is not automatically safer or more effective;
- accountability may be distributed across several actors;
- the EU AI Act is jurisdiction-specific;
- Gardner's delegation proposition is not a universal legal rule;
- the Accountability–Control Gap is organisational synthesis rather than a validated external scale.
