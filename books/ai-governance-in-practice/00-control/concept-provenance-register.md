# Concept Provenance Register

| Concept | Origin/status | Neighbouring scholarship / prior art | Conceptual gap | Exact claim permitted now | Claims excluded now | Hostile evidence / challenge | Revision trigger | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI Governance Operating System | Gardner organising metaphor — **not original as a label** | ISO/IEC 42001 AI management system; NIST AI RMF; several 2025–26 commercial platforms explicitly use “AI Governance Operating System”; 2026 SSRN “Governance Operating System” series | Our possible contribution is not the phrase but the specific organisational integration model linking strategy, decision authority, risk, data, controls, human judgement, capability, evidence and assurance | Use “operating system” as a disclosed metaphor for an integrated organisational governance model, provided the book differentiates it from software products and management-system standards | Do not claim first use, proprietary naming, empirical validation, or uniqueness of “AI Governance Operating System” | The metaphor may be read as software/platform language or as repackaged ISO management-system thinking | If architecture does not add a materially distinct organisational model beyond existing frameworks | REFRAME — retain metaphor, abandon naming originality |
| Governance Chain | Gardner synthesis — provisional | NIST Govern/Map/Measure/Manage; ISO management-system cycles; risk-control-assurance models; accountability and audit trails | Potential contribution is a practical traceability chain from an actual AI use through ownership, evidence and improvement | Use as an organising diagnostic sequence, not a universal law | Do not present as universally linear, causal, exhaustive or externally validated | Governance is iterative; controls, evidence and monitoring may be designed concurrently rather than sequentially | If mapping shows the chain adds no decision value beyond existing lifecycle models | CONDITIONAL — continue provenance mapping |
| Decision Rights Before Controls | **Established neighbouring idea; Gardner phrasing not yet distinctive** | Weill & Ross IT governance; MIT CISR 2026 Designing Decision Rights for AI; contemporary AI accountability frameworks | Stronger possible contribution: decision rights are necessary but insufficient unless authority, intervention capability, evidence access, competence and lifecycle accountability remain aligned | State that explicit decision rights are a foundational governance design requirement; develop originality around alignment and preservation of meaningful judgement | Do not claim that prioritising decision rights is novel, or that decision rights alone ensure accountability or control effectiveness | Formal authority can coexist with automation bias, deskilling, weak intervention capacity and symbolic oversight | Recast if evidence supports a better organising principle than rights-control-accountability alignment | REFRAME — move originality beyond decision-right allocation |
| Governance as Permission | Gardner synthesis — **promising but not wholly unprecedented** | Adler & Borys enabling versus coercive formalisation; Weill & Ross governance as both empowering and controlling; WEF responsible-AI innovation framing; current assurance/governance guidance linking confidence with adoption | Potential contribution: apply enabling-control theory specifically to AI governance and show how clear boundaries, escalation paths, evidence requirements and risk appetite can create legitimate room to act | “Well-designed governance can create conditions for more confident action by clarifying boundaries, authority and escalation.” | Do not claim governance automatically increases speed, ROI, innovation or trust; do not imply all formalisation is enabling | Governance can create bureaucracy, friction, false assurance and compliance theatre; enabling effect depends on design and context | If empirical evidence fails to distinguish enabling governance from merely more governance | RETAIN AS CENTRAL HYPOTHESIS — qualify and test |
