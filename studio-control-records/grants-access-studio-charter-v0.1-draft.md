## 1. Purpose and intended outcome

Grants Access Studio will help Australian applicants identify suitable grants, make informed decisions about applying, develop credible projects and prepare accurate, well-supported applications. It will also prepare successful applicants for grant obligations, delivery reporting and acquittal—the evidence that funding was spent and activities completed as agreed.

The Studio’s purpose is to improve application quality, suitability and efficiency. It cannot guarantee funding or determine a funder’s decision. A well-written application cannot compensate for ineligibility, unsupported claims or an undeliverable project.

This Charter defines a proposed operating design. It does not establish a live application portal, connect databases, schedule searches or authorise submissions. Those implementation activities will be recorded separately.

## 2. Users and decision rights

| User or role | Responsibility and authority |
|---|---|
| Studio owner — proposed: Alec Gardner | Approves the Charter, operating scope, significant changes and release readiness; appoints responsible people for each application. |
| Applicant owner | Confirms the applicant’s identity, organisational facts, project intent, evidence permissions and decision to pursue an opportunity. |
| Project lead | Owns project design, delivery feasibility, milestones, outcomes, resources and risk responses. |
| Application coordinator | Maintains the opportunity record, requirements matrix, deadlines, drafts, attachments and review actions. |
| Finance reviewer | Checks costings, contributions, payment timing, financial capacity and the program-specific treatment of GST and eligible expenditure. |
| Evidence and quality reviewer | Checks sources, claim support, coverage of criteria, consistency and unresolved gaps; challenges the draft from an assessor’s perspective. |
| Authorised applicant signatory | Approves declarations and the exact submission pack; separately approves any grant agreement or binding variation within their authority. |
| AI assistant | Searches, extracts requirements, maps evidence, drafts, checks and recommends. Does not certify facts, invent evidence, sign declarations or infer approval. |

One person may hold several roles where proportionate. Each role must still have a named owner. For applications involving substantial financial commitments, sensitive information or complex partnerships, appoint a reviewer who was not the primary drafter. The Studio owner’s approval does not replace a client’s authorised signatory.

## 3. Scope and applicant pathways

The proposed national scope covers Commonwealth, state and territory, local government and suitable philanthropic grants. Discovery may use aggregators, but opportunity requirements must be verified against the funder’s current materials.

| Pathway | Distinct checks |
|---|---|
| Business and enterprise | Entity type, ABN and other required registrations, location, trading history, turnover, staffing, project purpose, contributions and commercial outcomes. |
| Charity and community | Legal entity, charitable or not-for-profit status, DGR status where required, beneficiaries, community participation and any permitted auspice arrangement. |
| Research and collaboration | Eligible administering organisation, partners, research requirements, intellectual property, ethics and scheme-specific AI rules. Specialist review is required before use. |

Nexus BMG’s own applications and authorised client applications are proposed users of the same workflow. Their records, permissions and evidence remain separate. No applicant’s eligibility is assumed from a familiar business name or an earlier application.

In scope are readiness reviews, grant discovery, opportunity screening, funder-question drafts, project design, evidence research, budget preparation, application drafting, review, submission preparation, receipt capture, agreement-obligation tracking and learning from outcomes.

Out of scope are fabricated supporting documents, guaranteed success claims, lobbying or contact without authority, unapproved financial commitments, legal or tax determinations, and treating loans, procurement tenders or tax incentives as grants. These other funding mechanisms may be identified but must be routed to a separate process.

## 4. Workflow and required outputs

| Stage | Work performed | Required output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Establish readiness | Confirm applicant, decision makers, project intent, permissions and available documents. | Applicant profile, role assignments and missing-document list. |
| 2. Discover opportunities | Search official and suitable supplementary sources; verify round status and deadlines. | Opportunity register with official links, identifiers, location, funding range and last-checked date. |
| 3. Check eligibility and fit | Assess each mandatory condition, strategic alignment, preparation effort and delivery burden. | Eligibility matrix and apply, hold or decline recommendation. |
| 4. Design the project | Define need, beneficiaries, activities, outputs, outcomes, evaluation, milestones and partners. | Project brief and delivery plan. |
| 5. Assemble evidence and budget | Verify claims, gather applicant records, cost activities and check cash flow and contributions. | Evidence register, reconciled budget, risk register and attachment index. |
| 6. Draft the application | Answer the exact questions within limits, using evidence and the published assessment framework. | Application draft mapped to criteria and required attachments. |
| 7. Review and approve | Challenge the case, resolve contradictions and verify completeness, authority and consistency. | Review record, corrected pack and signatory approval tied to its version. |
| 8. Submit and record | Complete the authorised submission route and confirm receipt before the deadline. | Exact submitted copy, receipt/reference and submission timestamp. |
| 9. Manage the outcome | Record results and feedback; review any offer and convert accepted obligations into actions. | Decision record; for awards, approved agreement and reporting/acquittal calendar. |

Stages may iterate. New evidence or changed guidelines return the application to the affected review gate. Starting a project or incurring expenditure must be checked against that opportunity’s timing rules before any funding assumption is made.

## 5. Evidence controls

The Studio adopts the Nexus Evidence Engine sequence: **Search → Verify → Challenge → Register → Reuse → Monitor**. It uses the existing shared standard rather than creating a competing evidence system.

### 5.1 Source authority and currency

For each opportunity, retain the current round’s guidelines, application form, assessment criteria, FAQs, published amendments and relevant agreement terms. Record the version or publication date where available, retrieval date and exact source location. If documents conflict, log the conflict and seek an authoritative clarification through an authorised person; do not silently choose the convenient interpretation.

General guides inform practice but do not override the funder’s documents. Archived rounds, search snippets and aggregator listings are leads until verified. Recheck round status, amendments, submission instructions and closing date/time—including time zone—before submission.

### 5.2 Claim-to-evidence traceability

Each material claim must record a claim ID, wording, relevant criterion, source or applicant document, locator, date, verification status, limitations and responsible reviewer. Use the shared statuses verified, provisionally supported, unresolved or rejected. Clearly distinguish established facts, forecasts, proposed targets and interpretations.

Verify need and demand with evidence relevant to the actual location, population and project. Record survey sample sizes and periods; distinguish consultation from endorsement. Use a combination of evidence types where appropriate. Never create a quotation, partner commitment, beneficiary count, financial result or letter of support that has not been supplied or independently verified.

### 5.3 Applicant evidence and reuse

Reusable material may include entity documents, approved organisation descriptions, staff biographies, prior project results and financial records. Each item needs an owner, date, permission and review trigger. Revalidate it for each new application; prior acceptance by one funder does not establish acceptance by another.

Personal experience must not be represented as the applicant organisation’s completed work. Partner credentials must not be represented as the applicant’s credentials. Label projections and assumptions, including the basis for claimed productivity, participation or economic benefits.

### 5.4 Budget and delivery consistency

Every material budget item must map to a project activity and an eligible expenditure category, with a calculation or supporting quote. Reconcile totals across the form, budget and attachments. Separate confirmed and proposed contributions and cash and in-kind support.

Check program-specific rules for GST presentation, overheads, contingencies, related parties, procurement, duplicate funding, expenditure dates and contribution valuation. Do not turn guidance from one jurisdiction into a universal rule. Flag cash-flow exposure and unfunded ongoing costs for a human decision.

### 5.5 AI, privacy and integrity

Check the funder’s current AI and third-party assistance rules before drafting with AI. Record the assistance provided and meet any disclosure requirement. The applicant remains responsible for submitted content.

Use only applicant information authorised for the task, with appropriate handling of personal information, confidential commercial material and intellectual property. Restrict access by application and role. Define retention and deletion arrangements at intake using applicable agreements and requirements; this Charter does not impose a universal retention period.

## 6. Approval gates and stop conditions

Gate outcomes are **pass, hold or fail**, supported by reasons and evidence. A hold returns work for clarification or correction; a fail stops that route unless the underlying circumstances legitimately change. No deadline pressure overrides mandatory eligibility, truthfulness or signatory authority.

| Gate | Pass condition | Human decision owner |
|---|---|---|
| G0 — Authority and intake | Applicant identity, information permissions, roles and scope are confirmed. | Applicant owner |
| G1 — Eligibility and application decision | Mandatory conditions are evidenced; material uncertainties are resolved; the effort and commitments are acceptable. | Applicant owner or delegated decision maker |
| G2 — Project and financial feasibility | Project, outcomes, budget, contributions, risks and delivery capacity are credible and reconciled. | Project lead and finance reviewer |
| G3 — Evidence and application quality | All required criteria and attachments are covered; material claims are supported; contradictions and errors are resolved; limits are met. | Evidence/quality reviewer |
| G4 — Submission authority | The authorised signatory approves the exact version, declarations, requested amount and submission route. | Authorised applicant signatory |
| G5 — Submission completion | Receipt/reference and submitted copy confirm lodgement; absence of confirmation remains unresolved. | Application coordinator |
| G6 — Award acceptance | Agreement obligations, cash flow, risks and delivery commitments have been reviewed and accepted. | Authorised signatory, with project/finance review |
| G7 — Closure | Required reporting and acquittal are completed; outstanding obligations and funder acceptance status are recorded. | Accountable project owner and signatory where required |

An application is not ready for G4 with missing mandatory evidence, unresolved material eligibility, unsupported declarations, unexplained budget differences or absent authority. A material change after G4 reopens affected gates and requires approval of the revised submission version.

Routine research, drafting and reversible corrections may proceed under the agreed task authority without repeated permission requests. Submission, signing, external communications and financial commitments require explicit applicable authority. Existing clear authorisation remains valid within its stated scope; the Studio must not ask for it again unnecessarily.

## 7. Records and version control

The minimum record set comprises the applicant profile, opportunity register, requirements/eligibility matrix, evidence register, project plan, budget, risk register, attachment index, review/approval log, submitted pack and outcome/obligations record. They may be implemented as linked tables or document sections; separate systems are not required for each.

Assign stable identifiers to applicants, opportunities and applications. Store deadline time zones and responsible owners explicitly. Record who approved what, when, and against which version. Preserve submitted versions; later drafts must not overwrite the submission record.

Use v0.x for Charter drafts. Release v1.0 only after owner approval, recording date and scope. Maintain a change log and identify superseded versions. Where an authorised GitHub repository is selected, use it for controlled workflow assets; confidential applicant evidence requires separately approved access and storage arrangements.

## 8. Quality, testing and maintenance

Before operational release, run one real or explicitly simulated application through the workflow. Demonstrate that the Studio correctly handles an ineligible applicant, a missing attachment, an unsupported claim, a budget discrepancy, a deadline time zone and a changed guideline. Confirm it cannot report submission success without evidence of lodgement. Simulated approvals and submissions must remain labelled as simulated.

Measure preparation time, avoidable rework, evidence gaps, reviewer corrections, timely lodgement and completeness. Track funding outcomes with scheme and competition context; do not imply that funding success alone proves Studio effectiveness. Establish targets after the pilot provides a baseline.

Review the Charter after the pilot and when a material process, funder, privacy or AI-policy change affects it. Recheck individual opportunity documents at intake and before submission. These are operating requirements for the proposed Studio, not an assertion that automated monitoring has been activated.
