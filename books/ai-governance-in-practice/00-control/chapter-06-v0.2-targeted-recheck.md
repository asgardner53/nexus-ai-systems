## Three-Layer Evidence Test

### Evidence establishes

Authoritative and academic sources establish that practical AI governance depends on implemented organisational processes, data governance, explicit access arrangements, workflow design, documentation, logging, provenance, monitoring, change control, third-party governance and institutional accountability.

### Reasonable interpretation

These arrangements determine much of what an AI-enabled system can access, change, record and transmit, and whether an approved governance boundary can be observed, reviewed and revised in operation.

### Gardner synthesis

> **A governance boundary becomes real only when the operating environment supports, observes and preserves it.**

> **Governance does not live only in policies and committees. It is enacted in the data, identities, permissions, workflows, configurations, records and supplier arrangements that shape what people and systems can actually do.**

> **Infrastructure gives governance operational force, but it does not replace human judgement, institutional responsibility or assurance.**

The synthesis is original at the level of book integration. Its component ideas remain established prior art and are not presented as Alec Gardner discoveries.

## Contrary and boundary evidence survival

**PASS**

The revised manuscript retains that:

- access restriction does not establish legitimacy or sound judgement;
- provenance does not guarantee fairness or correctness;
- not every governance requirement should be hard-coded;
- documentation may be useful and still become stale;
- logs may be incomplete and create new risks;
- contracts cannot guarantee practical visibility or control;
- abrupt shutdown may harm interdependent operations;
- a technically implemented control may enforce the wrong rule;
- infrastructure cannot make an unwise boundary wise.
