## 4. Stage A — Chapter 1 retrospective Evidence Engine integration audit

### Research question

Does the controlled evidence adequately support Chapter 1's argument that organisations must govern AI participation in work and decisions, not merely maintain a tool inventory?

### Material claims to test

At minimum test:

1. AI can be embedded in ordinary software and workflows before it appears as a separate AI project.
2. A tool inventory alone is insufficient for meaningful governance.
3. AI risk depends materially on context of use and consequence.
4. Shadow AI creates a practical visibility problem.
5. Visibility is a prerequisite for proportionate deliberate governance.
6. Embedded AI does not automatically mean high risk.
7. The same general-purpose AI capability can require different governance in different uses.

### Required workflow

1. Extract every consequential claim from the controlled Chapter 1 manuscript.
2. Map each claim to the existing claims register and evidence pack.
3. Use Firecrawl to:
   - find current official and primary sources;
   - retrieve Australian Government, NIST or other authoritative guidance;
   - identify stronger or newer evidence where material;
   - search for contradictory or limiting evidence.
4. Use Scite to:
   - resolve academic sources by DOI;
   - inspect citation context and notices;
   - identify supporting and contrasting research;
   - record inclusion and exclusion decisions.
5. Return to official or publisher full text for claim-entailment verification.
6. Apply the Three-Layer Evidence Test.
7. Establish safe wording and unsafe overstatement for each claim.
8. Define monitoring only for sources that can materially change.
9. Determine whether the manuscript remains closed or requires a controlled reopening.

## 5. Stage B — Chapter 2 retrospective Evidence Engine integration audit

### Research question

Does the controlled evidence adequately support Chapter 2's distinction between policy, governance artefacts and operationally enforceable control?

### Material claims to test

At minimum test:

1. Policy is necessary but insufficient for enacted AI governance.
2. Formal governance can become decoupled from operational practice.
3. Operational governance requires roles, authority, controls, monitoring and feedback.
4. Instruction and enforceable control are different organisational mechanisms.
5. Visible governance artefacts can create false assurance when operational coupling is weak.
6. Static approval can become unreliable when assumptions or operating conditions change.
7. Formalisation can strengthen governance when it is internalised and connected to practice.
8. Weak implementation is not automatically deliberate governance theatre.

### Required workflow

Apply the same Firecrawl–Scite–full-text–challenge–register sequence used for Chapter 1.

Pay particular attention to:

- decoupling scholarship;
- NIST and ISO management-system requirements;
- contrary evidence showing that formalisation, policy, standards and training can work;
- the provenance of “false assurance” and the instruction/control distinction as Gardner diagnostic language;
- whether any board-level implication has been overstated as an empirical fact.
