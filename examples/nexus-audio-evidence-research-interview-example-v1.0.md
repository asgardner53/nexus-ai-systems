## 6. External verification plan

Each material attributed claim must be tested against independent evidence before publication.

### Claim A — Task automation vs job redesign

**Interview claim:** Organisations often automate tasks before redesigning the surrounding job.

**Verification required:**
- research on task-level automation and job redesign;
- implementation studies showing sequencing of automation and work redesign;
- practitioner evidence from organisations undergoing AI transformation.

**Challenge question:** Are there organisations that redesign roles first and automate second, and under what conditions?

### Claim B — Human work shifts toward judgement and coordination

**Interview claim:** AI adoption can shift human work toward judgement, exception handling and coordination.

**Verification required:**
- occupational/job-design research;
- human–AI collaboration studies;
- organisational case evidence.

**Challenge question:** In which jobs does AI reduce judgement demands rather than increase them?

### Claim C — Productivity gains do not imply reduced capability requirements

**Interview claim:** Productivity improvement does not automatically mean capability requirements decline.

**Verification required:**
- productivity studies;
- skills/capability research;
- evidence on task complexity and skill-biased technological change.

**Challenge question:** Are there contexts where capability requirements genuinely fall after automation?

### Claim D — Accountability redesign may lag

**Interview claim:** Managers may underestimate the need to redesign accountability when AI performs part of a workflow.

**Verification required:**
- governance and accountability literature;
- case studies involving human–AI decision responsibility;
- policy or standards material addressing accountability boundaries.

**Challenge question:** Is the issue lack of accountability redesign, or simply poor implementation discipline?

### Claim E — AI adoption as work-system redesign

**Interview claim:** Stronger implementations treat AI adoption as work-system redesign rather than software deployment alone.

**Verification required:**
- sociotechnical systems research;
- implementation case studies;
- organisational change evidence;
- workforce transformation research.

**Challenge question:** Does work-system redesign consistently improve outcomes, and what are the boundary conditions?

## 8. Example verification and challenge outcome

For this worked example, assume the subsequent Evidence Engine review finds:

- multiple independent sources support the proposition that AI and automation frequently reshape task composition rather than simply eliminate whole jobs;
- research supports the importance of human judgement, exception handling and coordination in many human–AI systems;
- evidence on skills is mixed: some roles experience capability expansion while others experience simplification or deskilling;
- governance literature supports the need for clear accountability where automated systems influence decisions;
- sociotechnical and work-design research supports treating technology adoption as a work-system issue, but outcomes depend on implementation context.

### Contrary and limiting evidence

The challenge review identifies important limits:

- work redesign effects vary substantially by occupation, task structure and organisational maturity;
- automation can reduce skill requirements in some settings;
- not every AI deployment requires wholesale job redesign;
- productivity improvement can occur without major structural redesign;
- expert interview evidence is useful for framing and interpretation but should not be treated as independent confirmation of the same proposition.

## 9. Supported interpretation

Following verification and challenge, the safe synthesis becomes:

**Supported interpretation:**

AI adoption is often most usefully understood as a work-design issue rather than a software-installation issue alone. In many settings, automation changes the mix of tasks performed by people and increases the relative importance of judgement, coordination and exception handling. However, the effect is not universal: some roles may be simplified, capability demands may fall in some contexts, and the extent of redesign required depends on the work, technology and organisational environment.

This interpretation is narrower and more defensible than the strongest form of the interview claim.

## 10. Publication wording control

### Unsafe wording

"AI always makes human work more complex and organisations that fail to redesign jobs will fail."

### Safer evidence-based wording

"AI adoption can change the mix of tasks within jobs and, in many contexts, increase the importance of judgement, coordination and exception handling. The scale and direction of that change depend on the work system, the technology and how implementation is managed."

### Attribution where the interview adds practitioner value

"The expert interviewed for this research described the strongest implementations as work-system redesign rather than software deployment alone."

Where used in publication, this sentence remains explicitly attributed unless independently verified as a broader factual proposition.
