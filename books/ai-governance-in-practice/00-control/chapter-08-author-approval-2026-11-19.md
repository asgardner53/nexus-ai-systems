## Approved chapter argument

The chapter establishes that governance is incomplete where it can identify concerns but cannot convert them into legitimate organisational permission.

Approved construct:

> **Governance as Permission is an organisational lens for designing governance so that people and systems can act within explicit, proportionate and reviewable boundaries, with defined authority, evidence requirements, exception paths, escalation and the ability to restrict or withdraw permission when conditions change.**

Approved governing propositions:

> **Well-designed governance creates legitimate room to act by clarifying what may proceed, within which boundaries, under whose authority, with what evidence and when escalation is required.**

> **The opposite of weak governance is not necessarily more governance. It is better-designed governance.**

> **Permission to act is not permission to ignore risk.**

Approved Chapter 9 handoff:

> **Permission without evidence becomes optimism.**

## Approved intellectual boundaries

The chapter may integrate established work on:

- enabling and coercive formalisation;
- decision rights and accountability;
- risk appetite, tolerance and proportionality;
- conditional approval and no-go decisions;
- exception handling and escalation;
- high-reliability organising;
- responsible innovation;
- red tape, administrative burden and workarounds.

The chapter does not present Governance as Permission as:

- the first theory of enabling governance;
- an externally validated instrument;
- a legal doctrine or compliance status;
- a maturity model or scoring method;
- proof that governance increases speed, trust, productivity, innovation, adoption or ROI;
- permission to ignore law, rights, safety or ethical limits;
- a transfer of institutional accountability to users, teams or AI systems.

The phrase *a kind of governance debt* remains lowercase descriptive language only and is not approved as a separate named construct.
