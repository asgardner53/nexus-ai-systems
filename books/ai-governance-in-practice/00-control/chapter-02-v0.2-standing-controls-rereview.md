# Chapter 2 v0.2 — Standing Controls Re-Review

**Chapter:** When Policy Exists but Control Does Not  
**Date:** 2026-11-01  
**Review basis:** Standing Manuscript Controls v1.0 + Platinum Gates 3–7  
**Decision:** **PASS DEVELOPMENTAL REVIEW → move to AUTHOR REVISION**

## 1. Argument

**Outcome: PASS**

The chapter sustains one dominant proposition:

> **Policy tells the organisation what it intends; governance determines what actually happens.**

The chapter does not drift into a generic critique of policy. Its sharper argument is that policy becomes governance only when it is operationally coupled to roles, permissions, controls, evidence, monitoring and behaviour.

That proposition is consistent with the book's standing question:

> How does an organisation govern AI as part of how it governs itself?

## 2. Architecture and flow

**Outcome: PASS**

The chapter progresses logically:

1. visible governance architecture creates confidence;
2. live operational questions expose uncertainty;
3. decoupling explains the policy–practice gap;
4. ISO/NIST show that mature governance already assumes implementation beyond policy;
5. practical mechanisms show where principles acquire force;
6. artefacts are distinguished from capability;
7. monitoring and feedback show why governance must remain active;
8. the chapter ends by exposing the human-accountability problem reserved for Chapter 3.

The chapter does not re-explain Chapter 1. It assumes visibility and advances the argument from **where AI participates** to **whether governance actually constrains and directs that participation**.

## 3. Evidence

**Outcome: PASS**

Material claims are visible and traceable in the controlled manuscript.

### Evidence surviving correctly
- Westphal & Zajac support the established organisational phenomenon of decoupling.
- de Bree & Stoopendaal support the work-as-imagined / work-as-done distinction.
- ISO/IEC 42001 is used to establish that AI management systems include policies, objectives, processes, implementation, evaluation and improvement.
- NIST AI RMF is used to establish operational governance requirements including roles, processes, training, monitoring and review.
- Papagiannidis et al. and Sayan & Bueechl are cited at the point where implementation difficulties are discussed.
- Stanford HAI 2026 is cited for current formalisation and implementation barriers.

No material research claim remains only in the research pack.

### Qualification retained
The manuscript explicitly states that the evidence does **not** establish that formal governance is ineffective.

This preserves the strongest contrary evidence and avoids confirmation bias.

## 4. Forensic Evidence Survival

The evidence survived the rewrite.

## 5. Doctrine integrity

**Outcome: PASS**

The manuscript keeps three layers distinguishable:

### External evidence
Decoupling scholarship, ISO/IEC 42001, NIST, implementation studies and Stanford AI Index.

### Gardner interpretation
The organisational reading that policy becomes governance through operational coupling.

### Emerging doctrine / author synthesis
> **Governance exists where principles alter decisions, permissions, controls, evidence and behaviour.**

> **Policy tells the organisation what it intends; governance determines what actually happens.**

These are not presented as formal AIRBOK doctrine or universally established definitions.

No draft doctrine is misrepresented as approved doctrine.

## 6. Originality

**Outcome: PASS**

The underlying concept of decoupling is established scholarship and is treated as such.

The chapter's originality lies in applying that evidence to the book's organisational operating-system argument:

- governance artefacts versus governance capability;
- operational coupling between intent and action;
- the movement from policy to permissions, controls, evidence, monitoring and behaviour;
- the bridge from operational governance into the human-accountability problem.

The manuscript does not claim discovery of decoupling, management systems or policy–practice gaps.

## 7. Practical value

**Outcome: PASS**

The chapter changes the leader's diagnostic question.

Instead of asking:

> Do we have an AI policy?

the reader is pushed towards asking:

> What changes in a real decision because this policy exists?

The customer-complaints example makes the distinction tangible without turning the chapter into a checklist or training manual.
