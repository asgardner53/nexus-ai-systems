## Overall judgement

The v0.2 expansion resolves the central developmental weakness identified in v0.1. At 2,454 words, the chapter now gives the Accountability–Control Gap enough organisational and intellectual space to feel earned rather than announced.

The expansion deepens the argument without importing Chapter 4's Meaningful Accountability Test or Chapter 5's full delegated-authority analysis.

## Argument

**Outcome: PASS**

The dominant proposition remains clear:

> **Delegation does not transfer accountability.**

The proposition is properly qualified as Gardner interpretation rather than a universal legal rule.

The deeper argument is sustained throughout:

> Accountability becomes fragile when the person or role carrying it no longer has enough practical control to exercise meaningful judgement.

The chapter does not drift into a generic human-in-the-loop discussion.

## Expansion test

**Outcome: PASS**

The additional material is substantive rather than padded.

### Out-of-the-loop analysis
The chapter now explains why the organisation may become more dependent on human intervention at the same time that routine automation makes that human less familiar with the work from which the exception emerged.

### Distributed responsibility
The chapter now distinguishes provider, data, system, managerial, review and executive contributions without turning responsibility into an undifferentiated list.

### Board and executive consequence
The chapter now shows why formal human approval is an assurance question, not merely a workflow detail.

### Accountability–Control Gap
The reader experiences the gap before it is named. The construct is now earned.

The ending retains forward pressure:
