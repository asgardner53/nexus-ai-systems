# Chapter 10 v0.1 — Argument, Final-Chapter Architecture, Narrative and Book-Prose Review

**Date:** 2026-11-19  
**Controlled manuscript:** `chapter-10-when-the-system-begins-to-act-v0.1.md`

**Decision:** **PASS — proceed to forensic evidence, theory, red-team and whole-book-closing review**

## Overall judgement

Chapter 10 v0.1 succeeds as the book's final integration chapter. It does not become speculative futurism, an agent-security manual or a new-framework launch. The continuing knowledge-assistant case brings the reader from a modest, apparently bounded action into a chain of local permissions whose combined organisational consequence was never consciously authorised.

The chapter extends the book's existing architecture rather than repeating it. Chapter 5 established delegated authority; Chapter 10 examines authority while it is being exercised through tools, handoffs and state changes. Chapter 9 established retrospective evidence and assurance; Chapter 10 asks whether evidence and intervention can operate quickly enough while action is still unfolding.

No structural rewrite is required. Any v0.2 revision should be local and evidence-led.

## Argument review

### Dominant proposition

**PASS**

The manuscript earns and sustains:

> **When AI can act, governance must be enforceable at the point where authority becomes action.**

The proposition appears only after the reader has experienced the shift from a documented permission to connected actions that create a wider consequence.

### Supporting proposition

**PASS**

The chapter develops:

> **Delegated action should remain bounded, attributable, observable, interruptible and revocable, with reversibility or defined recourse proportionate to consequence.**

The surrounding prose prevents this from becoming an abstract control slogan. It is grounded in the practical limits of stopping the first agent after tasks, schedules, notifications and supplier workflows have already changed.

### Unit of analysis

**PASS**

The chapter successfully shifts attention from the model or final output to the complete action path connecting identity, principal, authority, tools, state changes, counterparties, evidence and recourse.

The phrase *action path* remains descriptive lower-case wording and is not elevated into another named framework.

### Argument progression

**PASS**

The chapter develops logically:

1. one modest action permission appears legitimate;
2. the consequence widens through another agent and connected systems;
3. agentic capability is established as a spectrum;
4. output becomes organisational action;
5. identity is separated from current authorisation;
6. technical capability is separated from legitimate authority;
7. governance moves into the execution path;
8. human approval is bounded and prevented from becoming ritual;
9. intervention expands beyond a kill switch;
10. evidence and grant chains travel with material action;
11. observability is limited by scale and trace volume;
12. multi-agent safety is treated as a system property;
13. evaluation is bounded and must be revisited after material change;
14. adaptive governance remains institutional;
15. delegation returns to institutional accountability;
16. the four book propositions close in one organisational conclusion.

No movement is out of sequence or dependent on a concept not yet earned.

## Final-chapter architecture review

### Closure of the ten-chapter journey

**PASS**

The chapter closes the book's central movement:

- from AI use to participation in work;
- from policy to operational control;
- from nominal human ownership to meaningful accountability;
- from general tool approval to delegated authority;
- from authority to infrastructure and capability;
- from capability to permission;
- from permission to evidence and assurance;
- from retrospective assurance to action as it occurs.

The ending returns to the book's durable organisational obligation rather than predicting a distant technological future.

### Use of earlier propositions

**PASS**

The chapter recalls four established propositions sparingly and at the point where their relationship has become visible:

- capability does not confer authority;
- delegation does not transfer accountability;
- successful execution does not eliminate the need for assurance;
- governance must be enforceable where authority becomes action.

This is cumulative closure rather than a chapter-by-chapter recap.

### Relationship to the conclusion

**PASS**

The final line:

> **The organisation must still be able to govern what it permits AI to do.**

functions as the book's closing proposition. It does not open another technical problem or require an additional framework.
