## 2. Proposed Amazon book description — NOT YET AUTHOR-APPROVED

**Who is accountable when AI increasingly shapes the decisions an organisation makes?**

Artificial intelligence is moving beyond helping people write, analyse and recommend. It is becoming embedded in workflows, influencing decisions and, in some settings, taking action. Yet the people held accountable may have less visibility into how those outcomes arise—and less practical ability to challenge or stop them.

*AI Governance in Practice* examines this emerging gap between formal responsibility and meaningful control.

Alec Gardner argues that effective AI governance cannot be reduced to a policy, an approval step or a final human signature. It must operate through the organisation itself: connecting purpose, delegated authority, human judgement, capability, operating controls, evidence, monitoring and intervention.

Written for executives, directors, managers, HR and workforce leaders, and risk and transformation professionals, the book explores what it takes to keep accountability meaningful as AI becomes part of everyday work.

Its central question is deceptively simple: **What organisational conditions must exist for people to remain genuinely responsible for decisions increasingly shaped by AI?**

This is a book about building those conditions—not merely declaring that humans remain in control.
