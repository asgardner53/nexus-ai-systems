## Three-Layer Evidence Test

### Evidence establishes

- effective oversight requires practical competence, information, authority and intervention;
- responsibility, ability, authority and control should be coherently allocated;
- human judgement and automation effects are context-dependent;
- institutional design matters alongside individual attributes.

### Reasonable interpretation

- accountability assigned without these practical conditions is fragile or potentially ceremonial;
- the five conditions can be applied as an organisational diagnostic to a defined AI-mediated decision arrangement;
- a material weakness in one condition may undermine the practical usefulness of the whole arrangement.

### Gardner synthesis

> **Accountability should not outrun capability, visibility, authority or intervention capacity.**

> **Capability does not confer authority.**

> **The Meaningful Accountability Test** as a five-condition organisational diagnostic linked to the Accountability–Control Gap and governance operating system.

## Contrary evidence survival

The manuscript retains all material challenge boundaries:

- human involvement is not automatically beneficial;
- automation does not inevitably erode capability;
- transparency does not guarantee understanding;
- authority does not guarantee effective intervention;
- accountability may remain institutional even where direct control is distributed;
- a system may pass the diagnostic and remain unsuitable to deploy.
