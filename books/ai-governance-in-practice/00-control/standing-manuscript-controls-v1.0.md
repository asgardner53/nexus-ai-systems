# AI Governance in Practice — Standing Manuscript Controls v1.0

**Title:** AI Governance in Practice: Building the Operating System for Responsible, Accountable and Effective AI  
**Author:** Alec Gardner  
**Publisher:** Nexus BMG Pty Ltd  
**Status:** Controlled standing instruction set  
**Date:** 2026-11-01

## Core proposition

The book argues that **AI governance should be treated as an organisational operating system**, not as a policy document or technology control.

The operating system connects:

**strategy + work + authority + human judgement + accountability + data + controls + evidence + assurance + capability + learning**

Standing practical question:

> **How does an organisation govern AI as part of how it governs itself?**

## Source rules

The book must use **independent research and original analysis**.

- NAT11423007 material may be used only as research input, not manuscript source text.
- AIRBOK may inform the book, but the ebook must not reproduce AIRBOK doctrine or repository content.
- Evidence workflow: **Search → Verify → Challenge → Register → Reuse → Monitor.**
- Prefer primary and authoritative sources.
- Distinguish clearly between:
  - external evidence;
  - Alec Gardner's interpretation;
  - emerging AIRBOK doctrine;
  - formally approved doctrine.
- Draft doctrine must never be presented as formally approved.

## Writing voice

The manuscript must sound like **Alec Gardner**, not a generic consultant or AI-generated governance handbook.

Voice characteristics:

**workshop practicality + engineering discipline + educational clarity + academic rigour + commercial judgement + Gestalt awareness + executive experience + board-level perspective**

Use Australian English throughout.

Avoid:
- consultant-speak;
- AI hype;
- inflated claims;
- artificial profundity;
- excessive headings;
- generic lists;
- standards-manual prose;
- unsupported “research shows” statements.

House test:

> **If this could have been written by any competent consultant or generic AI, it isn’t finished.**

## Chapter form

Each chapter should read as a **real book chapter**, not an extended report.

Each chapter should:
1. open with a meaningful organisational tension;
2. establish one dominant proposition;
3. develop that argument through sustained narrative;
4. use evidence to strengthen the argument rather than overwhelm it;
5. distinguish evidence from interpretation;
6. show organisational and leadership consequences;
7. conclude by changing how the reader sees or acts on the issue.

Use lists and tables only when they genuinely improve comprehension.

## Governance operating system

Use selectively across the manuscript:

**Purpose → Intent → Authority → Boundaries → Human judgement → Execution → Evidence → Assurance → Accountability → Learning**

Do not dump the entire model into every chapter. Introduce only the elements required by the chapter argument.

## Approved manuscript propositions — emerging doctrine / author interpretation

These may be integrated into the manuscript as emerging doctrine or Gardner interpretation, not as universally established facts:

> **Capability does not confer authority.**

> **Delegation does not transfer accountability.**

> **Successful execution does not eliminate the need for assurance.**

The following remains under evidence development and must not yet be treated as settled:

> **Execution and assurance should be organisationally separable.**

## OpenAI evidence

October 2026 OpenAI incidents may be used as **case evidence**, not as the foundation or organising framework of the book.

They may illustrate:
- agents weakening monitoring;
- task authority versus system authority;
- correct outputs achieved through unacceptable pathways;
- the need for execution assurance;
- the difference between instruction and enforceable control.

The book must remain vendor-independent.

## Chapter workflow

Every chapter moves through:

**research brief → detailed chapter brief → first draft → argument/flow review → evidence verification → integrity review → author revision → copyedit → proof → publication approval**

Forensic Evidence Survival remains mandatory:

> **Did we improve the prose without accidentally stripping out the evidence that made the argument credible?**

## Review gates

Every chapter is tested for:

- **Argument** — one clear proposition.
- **Architecture** — logical progression without repetition.
- **Evidence** — material claims supported and current.
- **Evidence survival** — evidentiary backbone preserved through rewriting.
- **Doctrine integrity** — evidence, interpretation and emerging doctrine correctly separated.
- **Originality** — genuinely Gardner/Nexus BMG argument, not reformulation of existing frameworks.
- **Practical value** — changes how leaders understand or act.
- **Voice** — sounds like Alec Gardner.
- **Repetition** — avoids re-explaining earlier chapters.
- **Publication readiness** — citations, names, dates and claims defensible.
