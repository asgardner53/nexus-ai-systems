# Concept Provenance Register

| Concept | Origin/status | Neighbouring scholarship / prior art | Conceptual gap | Exact claim permitted now | Claims excluded now | Hostile evidence / challenge | Revision trigger | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI Governance Operating System | Gardner organising metaphor — **not original as a label** | ISO/IEC 42001 AI management system; NIST AI RMF; several 2025–26 commercial platforms explicitly use “AI Governance Operating System”; 2026 research uses related “Governance Operating System” terminology | Our contribution is not the phrase but the specific organisational integration model linking strategy, decision authority, risk, data, controls, human judgement, capability, evidence and assurance | Use “operating system” as a disclosed metaphor for an integrated organisational governance model, provided the book differentiates it from software products and management-system standards | Do not claim first use, proprietary naming, empirical validation, or uniqueness of “AI Governance Operating System” | The metaphor may be read as software/platform language or as repackaged ISO management-system thinking | If architecture does not add a materially distinct organisational model beyond existing frameworks | REFRAME — retain metaphor, abandon naming originality |
| Governance Chain | Gardner synthesis — provisional | NIST Govern/Map/Measure/Manage; ISO management-system cycles; risk-control-assurance models; accountability and audit trails | Potential contribution is a practical traceability chain from an actual AI use through ownership, evidence and improvement | Use as an organising diagnostic sequence, not a universal law | Do not present as universally linear, causal, exhaustive or externally validated | Governance is iterative; controls, evidence and monitoring may be designed concurrently rather than sequentially | If mapping shows the chain adds no decision value beyond existing lifecycle models | CONDITIONAL — continue provenance mapping |
| Decision Rights Before Controls | **Established neighbouring idea; Gardner phrasing not distinctive** | Weill & Ross IT governance; MIT CISR 2026 Designing Decision Rights for AI; contemporary AI accountability frameworks | Stronger contribution lies beyond allocation of rights: preserving alignment among authority, capability, evidence access, intervention capacity and accountability | State that explicit decision rights are a foundational governance requirement | Do not claim that prioritising decision rights is novel, or that decision rights alone ensure accountability | Formal authority can coexist with automation bias, deskilling, weak intervention capacity and symbolic oversight | Recast if the five-dimension alignment model proves derivative | REFRAME — absorb into broader alignment framework |
| Governance as Permission | Gardner synthesis — promising but not unprecedented | Adler & Borys enabling versus coercive formalisation; Weill & Ross governance as empowerment/control; current responsible-AI assurance guidance | Apply enabling-control theory specifically to AI governance and distinguish governance that creates legitimate room to act from governance that merely creates friction | “Well-designed governance can create conditions for more confident action by clarifying boundaries, authority and escalation.” | Do not claim governance automatically increases speed, ROI, innovation or trust | Governance can create bureaucracy, false assurance and compliance theatre | If empirical evidence fails to distinguish enabling governance from merely more governance | RETAIN AS CENTRAL HYPOTHESIS |
| Authority–Judgement–Accountability Alignment | **Gardner synthesis — candidate core framework** | Flemisch et al. 2012 ability-authority-responsibility-control; Calvert et al. 2022 meaningful human control; Endsley & Kiris 1995 out-of-the-loop performance; Elish 2019 moral crumple zones; Zhu et al. 2026 meaningful oversight; Australian AI safety guidance | Existing work treats meaningful human control, capability, authority and responsibility strongly, but the book can synthesise these into an organisational governance test for AI-enabled work: does the accountable human retain sufficient capability, information, time, authority and intervention capacity to exercise judgement? | Accountability is meaningful only where organisational design preserves the practical conditions required to exercise judgement and control | Do not claim the relationship between ability, authority and responsibility is new; do not claim every AI decision requires synchronous human approval | Existing meaningful-human-control literature already contains much of the conceptual foundation; human review can also create bottlenecks or reduce performance if badly designed | If further provenance shows an existing organisational model already combines all five dimensions in substantially the same way | PROMISING — develop as synthesis, not invention |
| Accountability–Control Gap | **Gardner synthesis / book-level diagnostic phrase — candidate** | Elish moral crumple zones; responsibility-gap literature; meaningful human control; out-of-the-loop research | Give executives a practical diagnostic for situations where formal accountability exceeds the real capacity to understand, intervene or control | Use to describe a mismatch between assigned accountability and practical control/capability | Do not present the underlying phenomenon as newly discovered | “Gap” may be too broad unless operationalised; distributed accountability may be legitimate | If it cannot be measured or diagnosed consistently | DEVELOP — possible reader-facing diagnostic |
