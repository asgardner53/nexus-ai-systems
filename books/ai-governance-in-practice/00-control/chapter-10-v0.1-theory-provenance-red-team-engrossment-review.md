# Chapter 10 v0.1 — Theory, No-New-Framework Provenance, Red-Team, Engrossment and Whole-Book-Closing Review

**Chapter:** When the System Begins to Act  
**Date:** 2026-11-19  
**Controlled manuscript:** `chapter-10-when-the-system-begins-to-act-v0.1.md`  
**Decision:** **PASS WITH FOUR LOCAL EVIDENCE REPAIRS**

## Overall judgement

Chapter 10 v0.1 successfully closes *AI Governance in Practice* without becoming a speculative-futures chapter, technical implementation guide or launch pad for another named framework. It brings the book's established constructs together under the pressure of delegated action occurring through tools, systems and other agents.

The final chapter preserves the core proposition that institutional accountability survives delegation. It acknowledges the legitimate value of bounded action, distinguishes identity from current authority, rejects ceremonial human oversight, broadens intervention beyond a kill switch, treats multi-agent safety as a system property and preserves the limits of evaluation and observability.

No theoretical, provenance, red-team, engrossment or whole-book-closing defect requires structural revision. The four citation-visibility items in the Evidence Survival Register should be closed through a targeted v0.2 revision, followed by the complete gate recheck.

## Theory and authenticity review

### Agentic AI as a spectrum

**PASS**

The manuscript does not rely on one universal threshold or treat *agentic* as a settled binary category. Planning, tool use, persistence, delegation, coordination and supervision remain variable dimensions.

The governance question is anchored to actual organisational consequence rather than marketing labels.

### Output versus action

**PASS**

The manuscript identifies the material shift correctly: an output becomes organisationally consequential through tools, credentials, state changes, messages, transactions, handoffs and downstream systems.

The action-path proposition does not imply that the model alone owns or controls the outcome.

### Identity, capability and authority

**PASS**

The chapter clearly distinguishes:

- identity and authentication;
- technical capability;
- current contextual authorisation;
- institutional legitimacy.

It restates *capability does not confer authority* under runtime conditions without repeating the full Chapter 5 framework.

### Runtime enforcement

**PASS**

The chapter establishes that material authority boundaries require controls near execution. It does not claim every governance judgement should be encoded as machine policy or that runtime enforcement makes a poor boundary legitimate.

### Human approval and meaningful intervention

**PASS SUBJECT TO E10-02**

The substantive treatment is strong. Human approval is selective and meaningful only where the reviewer has information, time, authority and a practical alternative. Approval is one control among several rather than a universal cure.

The intervention portfolio appropriately includes refusal, pause, cancellation, revocation, quarantine, rollback, fallback, containment, safe degradation, decommissioning and recourse.

### Observability and evidence

**PASS SUBJECT TO E10-03 AND E10-04**

The chapter asks for proportionate traces of identity, authority, tools, handoffs, state changes and consequences. It rejects indefinite retention, complete transparency and chain-of-thought as sufficient explanation.

The manuscript also recognises that excessive trace volume can weaken rather than strengthen practical oversight.

### Multi-agent systems

**PASS**

The safety of the arrangement is treated as a system property. The chapter does not claim interaction failure is inevitable and distinguishes direct control, influence, dependency and accepted residual risk.

### Evaluation and adaptive governance

**PASS**

Benchmarks remain context-bound. Pre-deployment evaluation is treated as a gate rather than a permanent assurance conclusion. Reassessment follows material changes in models, tools, permissions, prompts, data, memory, counterparties, integrations or consequence.

Adaptive governance remains institutional rather than an agent's power to expand or rewrite its own authority.

## Engrossment review

### Reader investment

**PASS**

The continuing knowledge-assistant case allows the reader to see how individually reasonable permissions accumulate into an organisational consequence. The absence of a spectacular failure strengthens rather than weakens the argument.

### Middle-chapter momentum

**PASS**

The chapter moves through identity, authority, runtime control, intervention, evidence, multi-agent interaction and evaluation without losing the central case. Sources enter when the reader needs the next distinction.

### Intellectual movement

**PASS**

The reader moves from believing a bounded toolset and named owner are sufficient to recognising that current authority and recourse must survive every consequential handoff.

### Emotional and organisational register

**PASS**

The tone remains sober and practical. The chapter avoids fear-driven futurism and places responsibility on organisational design rather than personifying or moralising the agent.

### Final pages

**PASS**

The closing paragraphs bring together the four propositions without reading as a summary list. The final sentence is earned by the entire manuscript:

> **The organisation must still be able to govern what it permits AI to do.**
