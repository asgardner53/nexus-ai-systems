## Three-Layer Evidence Test

### Evidence establishes

- automation can be allocated differently across decision functions;
- allocation effects vary by task and context;
- authority, ability, responsibility and control are distinct;
- authoritative governance frameworks require role clarity, bounded scope, monitoring, intervention, change control and decommissioning;
- human involvement is not automatically effective or preferable;
- accountability remains attached to organisational roles and decisions.

### Reasonable interpretation

- a product label or one autonomy score cannot fully describe organisational authority;
- capability and permission should be treated as separate governance questions;
- material authority should be bounded, monitored, revisable and revocable;
- static approval may cease to reflect authority exercised in practice.

### Gardner synthesis

> **Capability does not confer authority.**

> **Authority drift** is the gradual movement between approved authority and authority exercised in practice through changes in capability, access, workflow, purpose or reliance.

> **Delegated Authority Map:** Function → Permission → Boundary → Control → Recourse, with accountability across the arrangement.

## Contrary evidence survival

The controlled manuscript preserves the following limiting evidence:

- automation may improve performance and reduce workload;
- autonomy is not mechanically equivalent to risk;
- human approval may weaken rather than strengthen control;
- autonomy scales can remain useful for orientation;
- reversible actions may still create cumulative or distributive harm;
- revocation may require safe transition and continuity planning;
- a complete authority map does not prove that deployment should proceed.
