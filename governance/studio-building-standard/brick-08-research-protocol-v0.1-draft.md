## 8.1 Governing principle

Research in a Studio must be purposeful, controlled and sufficient for the decision or output being supported.

Research is not complete because a search returned results, because several credible sources were found, or because the evidence supports the preferred proposition. A Studio must define what question it is trying to answer, where it should look, what evidence would challenge the emerging conclusion, and what conditions justify stopping research.

The governing question is:

> **Have we searched widely and critically enough to justify proceeding with this level of confidence?**

## 8.2 Mandatory build instruction

Every Studio that performs material research must create a **Research Protocol and Research Control Record** before research workflows or research prompts are treated as operational.

The record must define:

1. the research purpose;
2. the research questions;
3. the claims, requirements or decisions the research must support;
4. the approved source hierarchy;
5. search scope and boundaries;
6. required primary-source searching;
7. prior-art or precedent searching where relevant;
8. contradictory-evidence searching;
9. search terms, concepts and alternate terminology where needed;
10. inclusion and exclusion criteria;
11. currency requirements;
12. jurisdiction or contextual limits;
13. source capture requirements;
14. evidence extraction requirements;
15. research-gap recording;
16. stopping rules;
17. escalation conditions;
18. human review requirements; and
19. research record retention.

## 8.3 Research-question rule

Research must begin with an explicit question or set of questions.

The Studio should distinguish between:

- **descriptive questions** — what is known, required or occurring;
- **causal questions** — what appears to cause or influence an outcome;
- **comparative questions** — how approaches, sources or options differ;
- **evaluative questions** — whether something meets a defined standard;
- **predictive questions** — what may happen under stated conditions;
- **interpretive questions** — what evidence or authority means in context; and
- **novelty questions** — whether an idea, argument or framework is genuinely distinctive.

The research strategy must fit the question type.

## 8.4 Search-before-answer rule

Where the Studio's purpose requires current, authoritative, niche, disputed or consequential information, it must research before asserting an answer.

Model memory may help formulate search questions or identify candidate sources, but must not replace required source retrieval and verification.

The Studio must not present recollection as research.

## 8.5 Source-priority rule

Research must follow the approved source hierarchy established under Brick 3.

Where a primary or controlling source exists and is reasonably accessible, the Studio should prefer that source for material claims over summaries of the source.

Secondary sources may be valuable for interpretation, context, critique or discovery, but must not silently replace higher-authority material where the decision depends on the primary source.

## 8.6 Prior-art and precedent rule

Where originality, novelty, design distinctiveness, doctrine, policy innovation, publication value or intellectual contribution matters, the Studio must deliberately search for prior art or precedent.

The research should seek:

- earlier expressions of the same idea;
- equivalent frameworks under different names;
- competing models;
- prior implementations;
- historical precedents;
- relevant patents or standards where applicable;
- existing academic or practitioner formulations; and
- arguments that would weaken a claim of originality.

The Studio must not confuse unfamiliarity with novelty.

## 8.7 Contradictory-evidence search rule

Research must include a deliberate attempt to find evidence that challenges the emerging conclusion where the consequence or reliance level warrants it.

The Studio should ask:

- What evidence would make this claim weaker?
- What credible sources disagree?
- Under what conditions does the proposition fail?
- Are there studies, cases or authorities producing a different result?
- Is there evidence of harm, failure, non-transferability or unintended consequences?

A search process that looks only for confirming evidence is incomplete.

## 8.8 Boundary-condition research rule

Where the Studio develops recommendations, frameworks, propositions or generalisations, research must examine where the conclusion does and does not apply.

This may include differences in:

- jurisdiction;
- industry;
- organisation size;
- population;
- culture;
- role;
- technology;
- maturity;
- operating environment;
- time period;
- implementation quality; or
- other contextual variables.

The Studio should not generalise beyond the evidence without making the boundary explicit.

## 8.9 Search-strategy rule

The research protocol should specify how searching will broaden before it narrows.

Where appropriate, the Studio should use:

- core concepts;
- synonyms and alternate terminology;
- exact phrases;
- related constructs;
- opposing terminology;
- jurisdictional variants;
- historical terminology;
- named authorities or institutions;
- citation chasing; and
- source-specific searches.

The strategy should reduce the risk that the research outcome is determined by the first wording used in the query.

## 8.10 Inclusion and exclusion rule

Where research volume is material, the Studio should define which sources are included and excluded.

Criteria may include:

- relevance;
- authority;
- methodology;
- recency;
- jurisdiction;
- publication status;
- language;
- population;
- context;
- evidence type;
- conflicts of interest; and
- duplication.

Exclusion criteria must not be designed merely to remove inconvenient contradictory evidence.

## 8.11 Currency rule

Where the answer can change over time, the research protocol must define how currency is checked.

The Studio should distinguish between:

- sources that remain authoritative until formally superseded;
- evidence where recency materially affects relevance;
- current-state information requiring fresh verification; and
- historical evidence used deliberately for context.

The newest source does not automatically outrank a still-current controlling source, but stale material must not be presented as current without verification.

## 8.12 Research-depth rule

Research depth must be proportionate to consequence, uncertainty, novelty and reliance.

A low-risk drafting task may require limited research. A regulatory conclusion, publication claim, strategic recommendation, assessment judgement or novel doctrine may require deeper and more adversarial research.

The Studio should define what triggers deeper research, including:

- high consequence;
- material uncertainty;
- weak or conflicting sources;
- novelty claims;
- emerging or fast-changing topics;
- broad generalisation;
- external publication;
- formal compliance claims; or
- a failed evidence gate.

## 8.13 Research-stopping rule

Research must have an explicit stopping condition.

The Studio should not stop merely because it has found evidence supporting the preferred conclusion. Nor should it continue indefinitely in pursuit of impossible certainty.

Research may be sufficiently complete when, as appropriate:

- the material research questions have been answered;
- controlling and authoritative sources have been checked;
- important alternate terminology has been searched;
- relevant prior art or precedent has been examined;
- contradictory evidence has been deliberately sought;
- material boundary conditions are understood;
- additional searching is producing diminishing informational value;
- unresolved gaps are visible and appropriately qualified; and
- the evidence is sufficient for the intended reliance level.

The stopping decision must be linked to evidence sufficiency, not fatigue.

## 8.14 Diminishing-return rule

The Studio may recognise diminishing returns where additional research repeatedly produces sources that are duplicative, lower authority, materially redundant or unlikely to alter the conclusion.

Diminishing returns must not be invoked while a known material gap, unresolved source conflict or significant contradictory line of evidence remains unexplored.

## 8.15 Research-gap rule

Research gaps must be recorded explicitly.

A gap may arise because:

- no authoritative evidence exists;
- evidence is inaccessible;
- available studies are weak or conflicting;
- jurisdiction is unclear;
- relevant data is unavailable;
- evidence does not cover the required population or context; or
- time or access constraints prevent further verification.

The Studio must distinguish a research gap from proof that no evidence exists.

## 8.16 Search-failure rule

Failure to find evidence is itself a controlled research outcome.

The Studio must record, where material:

- what was searched;
- where it was searched;
- relevant terminology used;
- what was not found;
- limitations of the search; and
- the effect on confidence or the decision.

The Studio must not convert "I did not find it" into "it does not exist" without justification.

## 8.17 Source-capture rule

Material research sources must be captured sufficiently to support Brick 7 provenance requirements.

Source capture should include, where appropriate:

- source identifier;
- title;
- author or issuing body;
- publication or effective date;
- URL, repository path or document identifier;
- version;
- jurisdiction;
- source class and authority status;
- date accessed;
- material evidence extracted;
- claims or requirements supported;
- contradictory relevance; and
- notes on limitations.

## 8.18 Evidence-extraction rule

Research notes must distinguish between:

- direct evidence;
- paraphrase;
- interpretation;
- inference;
- quotation;
- methodological limitation; and
- reviewer commentary.

The Studio must not allow research notes to blur source content and Studio interpretation.

## 8.19 Research reproducibility rule

For material research, another authorised reviewer should be able to understand how the search was conducted well enough to reproduce or challenge it.

This does not require every exploratory search to be logged in full. It requires sufficient record of the material research path, key searches, source selections, exclusions and stopping rationale.

## 8.20 Research-bias control rule

The protocol must actively reduce predictable research bias.

Controls may include:

- contradictory-evidence search;
- alternate terminology search;
- primary-source preference;
- independent second search;
- source diversity;
- explicit exclusion rationale;
- blinded or staged review where appropriate;
- separation of proposition formation from final evidence assessment; and
- human challenge review.

The Studio must not assume that automated research is inherently neutral.

## 8.21 Proposition-first risk rule

Where a Studio begins with a preferred proposition, framework or hypothesis, the research process must test rather than merely decorate it.

If the evidence materially weakens the proposition, the Studio must be capable of revising, narrowing or abandoning the proposition.

Research must not be used solely to backfill citations around a conclusion that has already been treated as fixed.

## 8.22 Research escalation rule

The Studio must escalate where:

- controlling sources cannot be located or verified;
- authoritative sources conflict materially;
- evidence quality is too weak for the proposed reliance;
- jurisdiction or applicability is unresolved;
- research suggests material safety, legal, ethical or compliance risk;
- the user requests a certainty level the evidence cannot support; or
- the research process identifies a need for specialist human expertise.

## 8.23 Research lifecycle rule

Research should move through controlled states where material:

**Question Defined → Search Designed → Sources Retrieved → Evidence Extracted → Contradictions Tested → Gaps Assessed → Sufficiency Determined → Research Closed or Escalated**

The Studio must not treat retrieval as equivalent to completion.

## 8.25 Gate 8 — Research Sufficiency Gate

A Studio may progress beyond Brick 8 only when the following questions can be answered **Yes** with evidence:

- Are the research questions explicit?
- Does the search strategy follow the approved source hierarchy?
- Have controlling or primary sources been sought where required?
- Has prior art or precedent been searched where originality or novelty matters?
- Has contradictory evidence been deliberately sought?
- Have material boundary conditions been considered?
- Are inclusion and exclusion decisions defensible?
- Has currency been checked where material?
- Is research depth proportionate to consequence and uncertainty?
- Are material gaps visible?
- Is failure to find evidence recorded honestly?
- Are material sources captured with sufficient provenance?
- Can source evidence be distinguished from interpretation?
- Can another reviewer understand the material research path?
- Is the stopping decision based on sufficiency rather than convenience?

If any material answer is **No**, the research protocol is incomplete or the research is not yet sufficient.

## 8.26 Evidence required to pass

Gate 8 requires:

- the completed Research Protocol and Research Control Record;
- at least one representative research question;
- a recorded search strategy;
- examples of source capture;
- at least one contradictory-evidence search;
- at least one recorded research gap or explicit statement that none material was identified; and
- a documented stopping rationale.

For high-consequence or publication-grade work, a hostile or independent review of the research process may be required.

## 8.27 Failure response

If Gate 8 fails:

- the Studio must not represent the research as sufficient;
- unsupported certainty must be reduced;
- missing primary or controlling sources must be sought;
- contradictory-evidence searching must be completed;
- material prior art must be investigated where relevant;
- research gaps must be recorded;
- propositions may need to be narrowed or revised; and
- downstream evidence, argument or output gates must remain blocked where they depend on incomplete research.

A Studio that stops research when it finds agreement is not conducting controlled research.

## 8.28 Human approval point

The accountable owner must approve the research protocol before it is treated as operational.

Additional subject-matter, academic, regulatory, legal, technical or professional review may be required where research supports consequential claims or specialised domains.

Human judgement is required where the stopping decision, evidence sufficiency, conflicting evidence or applicability cannot be resolved by the approved protocol alone.

Any material change to research depth, source requirements, novelty testing, contradictory-evidence rules or stopping thresholds requires reassessment of Brick 8 and affected downstream gates.

## 8.29 GitHub control record

For Nexus BMG Studios, the Research Protocol and Research Control Record must be retained in the authoritative GitHub repository or another specifically approved controlled system.

The controlled record should preserve:

- research protocol;
- search strategy rules;
- prior-art requirements;
- contradictory-evidence requirements;
- stopping rules;
- gap-recording rules;
- material protocol changes;
- approval records; and
- representative research records where appropriate.

Sensitive or licensed research material may remain in an approved secure location and be referenced through the evidence architecture.

## 8.30 Brick 8 completion rule

Brick 8 is complete only when the Studio can answer five questions for any material research task:

> **What question are we trying to answer?**
>
> **Where should we look, and why?**
>
> **What evidence could prove us wrong?**
>
> **What material gaps remain?**
>
> **Why is it reasonable to stop searching now?**

If the final answer cannot be defended, the research is not yet controlled.

---

## Brick 8 core rule

**Research is complete when the evidence is sufficient for the decision—not when the search happens to support the preferred answer.**
