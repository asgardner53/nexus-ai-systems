## Approved chapter argument

The chapter establishes that organisations must govern the specific operational authority delegated to AI rather than infer authority from product labels, system capability or one overall autonomy level.

Approved governing proposition:

> **Capability does not confer authority.**

Approved supporting proposition:

> **Delegation does not transfer accountability.**

## Approved original synthesis

### Delegated Authority Map

Approved structure:

> **Function → Permission → Boundary → Control → Recourse**

with **Accountability** as an overlay across the complete arrangement.

The map remains a practical organisational diagnostic. It is not represented as:

- a validated scientific instrument;
- a legal test;
- a compliance certificate;
- a universal autonomy taxonomy;
- a numerical maturity scale;
- a transfer of legal personhood, moral agency or institutional accountability to AI.

### Authority drift

Approved definition:

> **The gradual movement between the authority originally approved and the authority a system comes to exercise through changes in capability, access, workflow, purpose or reliance.**

The underlying change-control problem is evidence-supported. The term remains Gardner descriptive synthesis rather than validated external terminology.
