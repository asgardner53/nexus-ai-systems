## Proposed Amazon description

**Listing draft for approval.** The retailer text is a synthesis of the locked manuscript; it does not change RC1.

**Who remains accountable when AI begins to act?**

AI is entering organisations through the tools and workflows people already use. It shapes shortlists, recommendations, approvals and customer responses. As those systems gain the ability to act, an uncomfortable question follows: do the people held responsible still have the knowledge, authority and practical ability to govern what happens?

In *AI Governance in Practice*, Alec Gardner examines the gap between formal responsibility and meaningful control. He presents governance as an organisational operating system connecting purpose, human judgement, delegated authority, workforce capability, operating controls, evidence and intervention.

Drawing on organisational experience, published research and clearly identified composite cases, the book follows AI from its often unnoticed arrival in everyday work to its growing capacity to initiate consequential actions. It examines why policies can exist without effective control, why a human signature may provide little assurance, and why evidence matters after an apparently successful deployment.

Gardner's Meaningful Accountability Test, Delegated Authority Map and Evolved Governance Chain provide ways to examine the relationships among responsibility, permission, control and evidence. The discussion stays grounded in the practical realities of organisations, where decisions cross teams, systems and suppliers.

Written for executives, directors, managers, HR and workforce leaders, and risk and transformation professionals, this book asks what must remain within human and institutional reach as more work is delegated to AI.

Its central organisational principle is that **delegating work to AI does not make responsibility for that work disappear.**
