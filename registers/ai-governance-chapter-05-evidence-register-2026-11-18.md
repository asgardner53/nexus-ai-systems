## Claim register

### C05-001 — Capability does not confer authority

- **Claim type:** Supported interpretation / Gardner shorthand
- **Intended use:** Publication and controlled framework development
- **Primary evidence:** Parasuraman, Sheridan & Wickens (2000); NIST AI RMF Govern; Australian Guidance for AI Adoption
- **Supporting evidence:** Flemisch et al. (2011); Cavalcante Siebert et al. (2022/2023); OECD AI Principles
- **Contrary/limiting evidence:** Technical capability and organisational permission may be deliberately aligned in well-bounded automation; the distinction does not imply capability should be ignored.
- **Source quality:** A/B
- **Currency check:** 18 November 2026; monitor revised NIST AI RMF
- **Jurisdiction/population fit:** Direct for governance guidance; indirect but strong for human-factors literature
- **Verification status:** Verified as interpretation
- **Safe wording:** A system's technical capability does not by itself establish organisational permission to use that capability.
- **Unsafe overstatement:** AI has no authority unless a lawyer formally delegates it.
- **Notes/limitations:** “Authority” is organisational permission, not legal personhood or moral agency.
- **Human approval required:** Yes — approve Gardner shorthand.

### C05-002 — Authority is function-specific

- **Claim type:** Verified interpretation
- **Intended use:** Publication
- **Primary evidence:** Parasuraman, Sheridan & Wickens (2000)
- **Supporting evidence:** MIT CISR (2026); Endsley & Kaber (1999); human–autonomy teaming literature
- **Contrary/limiting evidence:** Some contexts use overall autonomy classes for practical regulatory or operational purposes.
- **Source quality:** A/B
- **Currency check:** Stable foundational evidence; checked 18 November 2026
- **Jurisdiction/population fit:** Cross-domain conceptual and empirical evidence
- **Verification status:** Verified
- **Safe wording:** AI participation should be examined across the functions it performs rather than represented only by one overall autonomy label.
- **Unsafe overstatement:** Every autonomy scale is invalid.
- **Notes/limitations:** Overall classifications may still be useful if they do not hide mixed functional configurations.
- **Human approval required:** No.

## Contradiction log

### CON05-01 — Higher autonomy can improve performance

- **Claims affected:** C05-005, C05-006
- **Evidence A:** Higher automation may weaken awareness or recovery in some tasks.
- **Evidence B:** Automation improves consistency, workload and performance in many bounded tasks.
- **Nature of conflict:** Context-dependent performance trade-off.
- **Safe-wording change:** Do not present lower autonomy or more human review as universally safer.
- **Resolution:** Incorporated into the research brief and map boundary.

### CON05-02 — Human approval can reduce rather than improve control

- **Claims affected:** C05-006
- **Evidence A:** Human oversight is widely used as a safeguard.
- **Evidence B:** Ritual approval, overload and time pressure can create weak or symbolic review.
- **Nature of conflict:** Formal involvement versus substantive judgement.
- **Safe-wording change:** Match human involvement to function and context; do not require approval mechanically.
- **Resolution:** Incorporated.

### CON05-03 — Autonomy scores versus function-specific mapping

- **Claims affected:** C05-002, C05-010
- **Evidence A:** Many frameworks use autonomy levels for practical communication.
- **Evidence B:** Functional automation can be mixed, so one score can obscure actual permissions.
- **Nature of conflict:** Simplicity versus diagnostic accuracy.
- **Safe-wording change:** Do not reject autonomy levels outright; use the map where consequential detail matters.
- **Resolution:** Incorporated.

### CON05-04 — Revocation can create harm

- **Claims affected:** C05-008
- **Evidence A:** Systems should be overrideable, repairable and decommissionable.
- **Evidence B:** Abrupt termination can disrupt critical or interdependent services.
- **Nature of conflict:** Control versus continuity.
- **Safe-wording change:** Require safe, planned and proportionate revocation with alternative pathways.
- **Resolution:** Incorporated.
