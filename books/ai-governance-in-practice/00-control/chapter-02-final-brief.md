# Chapter 2 Final Brief — When Policy Exists but Control Does Not

**Status:** Final brief approved for drafting  
**Target length:** 2,400–3,000 words  
**Book role:** Move the reader from visibility of AI use to the deeper distinction between governance artefacts and enacted governance.

## Reader entry belief

If the organisation has an AI policy, principles, a committee, an approval process and perhaps a register, then it has governance.

## Opening contradiction

An organisation can possess all the visible signs of governance and still be unable to answer basic operational questions:

- Who can approve this use?
- Who can stop it?
- What evidence is required?
- What happens when conditions change?
- Who monitors whether the control still works?

The documents exist. Control remains uncertain.

## Chapter purpose

Show that policy, principles and formal structures are necessary but insufficient. Governance becomes real when they are coupled to decisions, roles, permissions, controls, evidence, monitoring and behaviour.

## Governing argument

**Policy tells the organisation what it intends. Governance determines what actually happens.**

The issue is not policy versus no policy. It is the degree of coupling between formal governance and operational practice.

## Reader discovery

The reader should come to see that “governance” is not a collection of artefacts. It is an organisational capability that must alter what people and systems are permitted, required and able to do.

## Evidence to integrate

- ISO/IEC 42001 — policies, objectives and processes; implementation, performance evaluation and continual improvement.
- NIST AI RMF Govern — implemented policies, processes, procedures and practices; accountability structures; training; monitoring; review.
- OECD AI Principles — traceability, lifecycle accountability and systematic risk management.
- Decoupling scholarship — formal policy adoption can become weakly connected to practice.
- Current AI governance implementation studies — continuing translation gap between principles and practice.
- Stanford AI Index 2026 — increasing formalisation alongside continuing implementation barriers.

## Gardner synthesis

Primary:
> **Governance exists where principles alter decisions, permissions, controls, evidence and behaviour.**

Secondary:
> **Policy tells the organisation what it intends. Governance determines what actually happens.**

Deeper line:
> **The gap between policy and practice is not an AI problem. AI simply makes the cost of that gap harder to ignore.**

## Narrative movement

1. Open in an organisation that appears well governed on paper.
2. Ask operational questions that expose uncertainty.
3. Establish policy as necessary, not the villain.
4. Introduce the policy–practice gap through organisational decoupling.
5. Show why AI magnifies the consequences of weak coupling.
6. Bring in NIST/ISO to demonstrate that mature governance already assumes implementation, accountability, monitoring and improvement.
7. Introduce the idea of governance artefacts versus governance capability.
8. End with the unresolved question: when responsibility is assigned to a human, what if that human no longer has meaningful control?

## Concepts to introduce

- governance artefact
- enacted governance
- policy–practice gap
- decoupling
- operational coupling

Use these as narrative language, not a glossary.

## Concepts deliberately withheld

- Accountability–Control Gap
- Meaningful Accountability Test
- delegated authority
- Governance as Permission

## Red-team boundary

Do not write:
- “policies do not work”;
- “governance committees are useless”;
- “formalisation is theatre” as a general claim;
- “more controls are always better”;
- “implementation gaps are deliberate”.

## Ending requirement

End on the human accountability problem without explaining it. Chapter 3 must feel unavoidable.
